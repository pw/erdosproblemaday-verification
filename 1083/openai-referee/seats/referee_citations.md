**2. Footnote 1 / reference [2]: accurate but unlocatable. COSMETIC.**
Location: p. 2 footnote 1; p. 102 entry [2].
Verbatim: "An earlier manuscript of Aksoy Yazici claimed the constant-factor bound in all dimensions d ≥ 3, but was withdrawn by the author because the proof of its Theorem 1.2 is not correct [2]." / "[2] Esen Aksoy Yazici. Erdős distance problem in Rd, 2020. Withdrawn by the author on February 12, 2020."
Source: arXiv:2002.01248 (v1 4 Feb 2020, v2 9 Feb 2020, v3 12 Feb 2020 "(withdrawn)"); arXiv comment, verbatim: "The paper is withdrawn as the proof of Theorem 1.2 is not correct". v2 abstract: "we prove Erdős distance conjecture in R^d, namely, a set of n points in R^2 determines Ω(n/√log n) distances, and for d ≥ 3, a set of n points in R^d determines Ω(n^{2/d}) distinct distances."
So the footnote's three facts (claimed c·n^{2/d} for d ≥ 3; withdrawn; reason = Theorem 1.2) are correct and the withdrawal date matches v3. Omissions: the arXiv identifier is not given (the only reference without a locator); the manuscript also claimed the planar Ω(n/√log n) (and v1 claimed Ω(n^{2/d}) for every d, which is false at d = 2 by the grid).
What [2]'s Theorem 1.2 is (read in v1 and v2, 3–4 pp.): a Cohn–Elkies-type linear-programming inequality — for an "admissible" f with f(x) ≥ 0 for |x| ≥ 1, f̂ ≤ 0, f(0) = f̂(0) = −1, every L-periodic set E has "distance density" |E/L|/|L| ≥ 1 — proved by Poisson summation "similar to the proof of [Cohn–Elkies, Theorem 3.1]", with f = −g for Viazovska's 8-dimensional magic function (Theorem 1.3). Theorem 1.1 then asserts, without argument, "We will assume that E is L periodic since this will give the minimum number of distances" and "if E is in a general position, then |Δ(E)| is greater than [|E/L|/|L|]·n^{2/d}". The stated reason for withdrawal is only "the proof of Theorem 1.2 is not correct"; visibly, step (1.1) uses hypothesis 1 (f ≥ 0 off the unit ball), which needs a minimum-separation (packing) hypothesis the statement does not make, and the passage from distance density to a distance count is not argued.
Resemblance to the present paper: none. [2] is Fourier/linear-programming on periodic sets with an 8-dimensional magic function; the present paper is a polynomial-method / rigid-motion-flat argument. No step, lemma or notation is shared.
Fix: add "arXiv:2002.01248" to [2].

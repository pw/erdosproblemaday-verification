The world, 14 October. The problem page records nothing of this: "5 ≤ χ ≤ 7", de Grey credited for the lower bound, the proof-claims tab empty, commenting suspended site-wide, last edited 22 January. The teorth database says open (last updated 31 August 2025). Bloom's 8 October post lists #508 ("This proof has been formalised. The PDF is 62 pages long.") and marks it "(partial)" on the FrontierMath list, with his caveat that he has "not yet looked at in any serious way" any of the proofs; none of the comments under it touches #508. Our brief assumed nobody had rebuilt the Lean, and that had been out of date for a week. Formal Conjectures merged HadwigerNelsonAtLeastSix as research solved on 11 October (PR #6898, by source inspection, a sorry grep and the comparator config — not a rebuild), while the exact-value statement stays open. Five third parties record machine checks: plasma-ai's claim card (7 October: "kernel-checked Lean proofs were built and axiom-audited here"); Sergi Galán's repository (decalion89, 9–10 October), which reports all 70 modules built and kernel-replayed and goes further — patches that run the same proof over the constructible, origami and radical fields, with notes listing the same four uses of square roots and the same uses of divisibility our seats found; cert-machine's comparator run (10 October, two GitHub Actions runs: the default Lean kernel and the nanoda kernel both accept the solution, with a control that fails); AI-hard's Comparator run at fd4aeeb (10–11 October, pass); and Li–Su (arXiv:2610.15424, 11 October), who rebuild the transfer half because they build a theorem on it: a single-class version of the transfer gives the first half of the Dúcz–Varga conjecture on the independence ratio of unit-distance graphs (#1070), "its only non-classical input" being Theorem 2.3. James Davies (arXiv:2610.12301, 8 October, two days after the release) proves by a different lattice method that, for finite distance sets with rational squares, χ(ℝ⁴) = χm(ℝ⁴) and, for d ≥ 5, χ(ℚd) = χ(ℝd) = χm(ℝd) — the full measurable coincidence in high dimension, stronger than Theorem 1.3's weak-measurable one — and conjectures χ(ℝ²) = 6; so Theorem 1.3 is new in the plane, with Davies's as the concurrent high-dimensional statement. Ying Xie's survey of the release (arXiv:2610.17090, 12 October) classifies the transfer as new ("the initial literature comparison found no matching transfer theorem"), and Davidson (arXiv:2610.09474, 7 October) already uses the result as an input. Public reaction is real and thin: Zelinsky on Bluesky the night of the release ("it doesn't look like the method is a direction that the prior lit used to my knowledge"), JoshuaZ on LessWrong (the Bachoc et al. citation gap), Dmitry Rybin on X ("totally unbelievable alien math?", 260,000 views), Gil Kalai's 7 October round-up (a report), and a MathOverflow question of 13 October asking for an explicit finite unit-distance graph that is not 5-colourable (score 22, no answer; the proof is existential by de Bruijn–Erdős, and the smallest known non-4-colourable graph has 509 vertices). We found no mention of it in Tao's or Aaronson's posts on the release; Hacker News has nothing on it at all (five queries, with positive controls). What no one has published in eight days is a referee reading of the human-readable argument; the closest public engagement with Payne's obstruction — without naming it — is Galán's field notes, which list the same uses of square roots our seats found, publish the same 3-adic counterexample over ℚ(i) that our §2–3 seat built, and re-run the Lean over smaller fields. One consequence the paper never states, found by the sources seat: Theorem 1.4 on its own gives χm(ℝ²) ≥ 6 — a measurable proper five-colouring is a weak measurable one — the first improvement on Falconer's 1981 measurable bound, and it does not depend on the transfer at all.

The problem (erdosproblems.com/508; Erdős 1961, 1975, 1981). What is the chromatic number χ of the plane — the fewest colours that colour ℝ² so that no two points at distance exactly 1 share one? The Hadwiger–Nelson problem (Nelson 1950). The page's bounds are 5 ≤ χ ≤ 7 — the hexagonal seven-colouring, and de Grey's 2018 finite unit-distance graph that is not four-colourable, which ended a sixty-eight-year stand at four (1,581 vertices, later cut to 553 by Heule and 509 by Parts, per the paper's introduction). The page also records the fractional bound ≥ 4 (Matolcsi–Ruzsa–Varga–Zsámboki) and Croft's upper bound 4.359, and says nothing of the measurable problem, where Falconer proved in 1981 that colour classes required to be Lebesgue measurable need at least five colours — thirty-seven years before anyone could say so without the measurability. The claim. OpenAI's 23 September 2026 preprint "The Euclidean plane is not five-colorable" (62 pages, family 158 of the 6 October release; on Bloom's 8 October list of 25 as "This proof has been formalised. The PDF is 62 pages long.", and marked "(partial)" on the FrontierMath Erdős list because the page asks for the exact value). Theorem 1.1: every colouring of the plane with five colours, with no regularity assumption whatever on the colour classes, has two points at distance one of the same colour; so 6 ≤ χ(ℝ²) ≤ 7. If this holds it is the first movement of the lower bound since de Grey, and it decides the problem up to one colour. The proof has two parts, and the first answers, in its weak form, a question open since Falconer. Theorem 1.3 (transfer): for every k, in ZFC, a proper k-colouring of ℝ² exists if and only if a weak measurable k-colouring exists — a Lebesgue-measurable c with ∑i ∫B(0,R) ∫S¹ 1Ai(x) 1Ai(x+u) dσ(u) dx = 0 for every R, i.e. monochromatic unit pairs of product measure zero, exceptional pairs allowed. Theorem 1.4: no weak measurable five-colouring exists. The paper is explicit that the two notions cannot be identified for free: it cites Payne's example of the graph on ℝ² whose edges join points whose displacement is a unit vector with rational coordinates, which is two-colourable but needs at least five colours measurably — so the transfer must use something the rational directions lack.

The problem (erdosproblems.com/508; Erdős 1961, 1975, 1981). What is the chromatic number χ of the plane — the fewest colours that colour ℝ² so that no two points at distance exactly 1 share one? The Hadwiger–Nelson problem (Nelson 1950). The page's bounds are 5 ≤ χ ≤ 7 — the hexagonal seven-colouring, and de Grey's 2018 finite unit-distance graph that is not four-colourable, which ended a sixty-eight-year stand at four (1,581 vertices, later cut to 553 by Heule and 509 by Parts, per the paper's introduction). The page also records the fractional bound ≥ 4 (Matolcsi–Ruzsa–Varga–Zsámboki) and Croft's upper bound 4.359, and says nothing of the measurable problem, where Falconer proved in 1981 that colour classes required to be Lebesgue measurable need at least five colours — thirty-seven years before anyone could say so without the measurability. Zero proof claims; one comment (BorisAlexeev, October 2025, noting that Erdős's 1975 "F(2) ≤ 6" was almost certainly a slip for 7); last edited 22 January 2026. The teorth database says open (last updated 31 August 2025).

The claim. OpenAI's 23 September 2026 preprint "The Euclidean plane is not five-colorable" (62 pages, family 158 of the 6 October release; on Bloom's 8 October list of 25 as "This proof has been formalised. The PDF is 62 pages long.", and marked "(partial)" on the FrontierMath Erdős list because the page asks for the exact value). Theorem 1.1: every colouring of the plane with five colours, with no regularity assumption whatever on the colour classes, has two points at distance one of the same colour; so 6 ≤ χ(ℝ²) ≤ 7. If this holds it is the first movement of the lower bound since de Grey, and it decides the problem up to one colour. The proof has two parts, and the first answers, in its weak form, a question open since Falconer. Theorem 1.3 (transfer): for every k, in ZFC, a proper k-colouring of ℝ² exists if and only if a weak measurable k-colouring exists — a Lebesgue-measurable c with ∑i ∫B(0,R) ∫S¹ 1Ai(x) 1Ai(x+u) dσ(u) dx = 0 for every R, i.e. monochromatic unit pairs of product measure zero, exceptional pairs allowed. Theorem 1.4: no weak measurable five-colouring exists. The paper is explicit that the two notions cannot be identified for free: it cites Payne's example of the graph on ℝ² whose edges join points whose displacement is a unit vector with rational coordinates, which is two-colourable but needs at least five colours measurably — so the transfer must use something the rational directions lack.

The architecture runs in two halves that meet at Definition 1.2, and the first half is where the novelty sits. Transfer (§2–4). Let F be the real algebraic numbers, E = F(i) the countable algebraic plane, K = {u ∈ E : |u| = 1} the countable group of algebraic rotations — dense in the circle and divisible. Restrict the given five-colouring (any colouring at all) to E; the group E ⋊ K is amenable, so Følner averaging of a point mass on the compact space of proper labelings of E gives an invariant probability law µ on proper labelings (Proposition 4.2's first paragraph — a Følner sequence and a weakly convergent subsequence, a textbook construction; the axiom of choice enters elsewhere, through the invariant mean on F/ℤ of §2.2, and in the converse direction through its compactness step and de Bruijn–Erdős). Let fi be the indicator that the origin has label i; properness says ⟨fi, Tu fi⟩ = 0 for every algebraic unit u. Lemma 4.1: the vectors on which the translation action is norm-continuous form L² of a factor, so projection onto them is a conditional expectation (it keeps 0 ≤ pi ≤ 1 and ∑ pi = 1), and on that factor the E-translations extend to a strongly continuous unitary action of all of ℝ². The remainder bi = fi − pi has a K-invariant spectral measure on the dual group D of Edisc giving zero mass to the Borel subgroup C ≅ ℝ² of continuous characters — a wild law — and the paper's central new statement, Theorem 2.3 (rigidity), says every wild K-invariant probability on D is Haar measure, whose Fourier coefficients vanish at every nonzero point of E. So the remainder is orthogonal to all its nonzero algebraic translates, the cross terms vanish, and ⟨pi, Tu pi⟩ = 0 survives for algebraic u, hence by strong continuity and density for every unit direction, hence by unitarity for every pair (x, x+u). Jointly measurable versions of x ↦ Txpi, Tonelli, one sample ω, and "take the least label with positive field" give a weak measurable five-colouring. Theorem 2.3 itself is proved in §2–3 by the Furstenberg–Zimmer compact-extension method: along a compact tower of an ergodic K-system the difference of two conditionally independent characters stays wild (Lemma 2.7, relative singularity); its Fourier coefficient is nonnegative and radial; two identities among sums of algebraic unit directions (Lemma 3.3) give a difference inequality for that coefficient; a number-field general-position lemma (3.4) and an "addition under a cap" lemma (3.5) turn any positive value at one nonzero radius into a uniform positive lower bound on a whole interval of algebraic radii, which the vanishing line averages of Lemma 2.5 forbid.

Why this cannot be a free lunch is the first thing a reader checks, and the paper names the obstacle itself: Payne's rational-direction graph is two-colourable yet measurably needs five colours, so the same words cannot work for Q(i) in place of F(i). They don't, and one can see where without opening the proof: the rational analogue of Theorem 2.3 is false. Every rational point of the unit circle has a reduced denominator whose prime factors are all ≡ 1 (mod 4), so the ring the rational rotations generate is R = ℤ[1/p : p ≡ 1 (mod 4)][i], a proper subring of Q(i) (1/3 is not in it) with infinite index. Its annihilator in the dual of Q(i)disc is an infinite closed subgroup H, invariant under rational rotations; Haar measure on H is a rotation-invariant probability, gives the continuous characters mass zero (a continuous character trivial on the dense set R is trivial), and is not Haar on the whole dual. For E = F(i) no such subgroup exists: every real algebraic x with |x| ≤ 2 is 2cos θ for an algebraic unit eiθ, so the ring generated by K is all of E and a K-invariant subgroup is an ideal of the field — zero or everything. That is where square roots in F do their work — they make the ring generated by K all of E; density of the directions is not it (the rational directions are dense too), nor is divisibility of K on its own — and it is also why the theorem is a statement about the plane and not about a lucky countable graph.

Obstruction (§5–8), for five labels and a weak measurable colouring. At a fixed centre x, weak-* limits of the typical circle samples e ↦ (1Ai(y + re))i as y → x and r → 1 define traces; the palette at (x, e) collects the labels with positive weight at e in some trace. Smooth transport of two unit directions gives exclusions between palettes (Lemma 5.2, Corollary 5.4's sixty-degree exclusion), a separation constant for centres whose palettes meet a fixed pair (Lemma 5.5), and Proposition 5.6: at every centre almost every palette has at most two labels. §6 records label transitions in a finite graph at each centre and proves (Theorem 6.5) that the centres whose graph contains a cycle form a closed locally finite set — the estimates run on the decay of the Fourier transform of circle measure, with no assumption of finite perimeter or smooth boundaries. §7 smooths the indicators by disk averages, thresholds and renormalizes, obtaining outside disks of vanishing total radius a continuous map into the complete graph K5 as a one-complex (at most two positive coordinates), fills the holes continuously in the forest regions, and uses a planar cover obstruction (Lemma 7.5: a closed square of side ten has no relatively open cover of mesh at most two and multiplicity at most two — the Lean's PlanarCover.compact_open_cover_impossible states it for any compact set containing the open disk of radius 5, by a winding argument where the paper uses Brouwer) to force a nontrivial loop around one cyclic-centre disk; a simple cycle in its reduced edge path, a connected component of each edge midpoint's preimage crossing the annulus, and limits give Proposition 7.1: compact connected sets Kj with a common point x, forming a cycle of labels, with both labels of Kj absent almost everywhere from Δ(Kj) = {z : minq∈Kj |z − q| < 1 < maxq∈Kj |z − q|} (the interface mechanism of Sokolov–Voronov, here derived from measure rather than assumed from polygons). §8 kills the three possible cycle lengths: five because six equal steps around a rotation orbit cannot close modulo five (Proposition 8.2), four by the parity of the six-position angular words of Lemma 8.3 together with the arc geometry of Lemma 8.4 (Proposition 8.5), three by six alternating sectors that leave an open region using only the three cycle labels almost everywhere (Lemma 8.8), into which a rational placement puts all seven vertices of the Moser spindle (Lemma 8.9, Proposition 8.10) — the spindle has no proper three-colouring, and typical points — density-one points of their own class, which Lemma 4.3 shows contain no unit pair, the one piece of §4 that Theorem 1.4 itself needs — are properly coloured.

What the seats found. Seven tool-equipped referee seats, each briefed to refute — five on disjoint stretches of the paper (§2–3 · §4 with the Lean's transfer · §5–6 · §7 · §8), one on the whole paper as a dependency graph with the Lean statement, one on sources and the world — between them covered all 62 pages from the rendered PDF, each scoped seat line by line through its stretch, and every one returned HOLDS AS READ: no FATAL, no GAP, a short list of cosmetic and exposition notes. Each seat rebuilt something and made it fail on purpose. The §2–3 seat verified the two unit-direction identities of Lemma 3.3 exactly in sympy (controls L → 2L, u → 2u fail), Lemma 3.4 exactly over ℚ(√2, i), Lemma 3.5 on 596 grid cases (the stronger bound w ≤ M fails in 109 of them), every constant in Lemma 2.7, and found the ergodic decomposition the brief suspected was missing written out on p. 22 — and it put its finger on the sentence that separates the algebraic plane from Payne's rational one: p. 13, in the proof of Lemma 2.7, "Each |xj| < 1 is a sum of two elements of K … The square roots belong to F", which is what forces ℤ[K] = E and fails for ℚ (1 − x²/4 must be a rational square; it is for 5 of 1,101 small rationals), with the same property used again on pp. 18, 19 and 21 and the divisibility of K only for the power maps of §3.2–3.3; it then built a 3-adic counterexample (3 is inert in ℤ[i], so rational rotations act isometrically on ℚ3(i); Haar measure on the closure of the rotation orbit of 1 there is invariant, ergodic, gives C mass zero and has Fourier coefficient 1 at z = 1) on which Lemma 2.7 fails at exactly that sentence and nowhere earlier, and noted Payne's two-colouring is 2-adic (colour by x + y mod 2; every rational unit step flips it, 1,260 vectors checked). The §4 seat checked Lemma 4.1 and Proposition 4.2 line by line, read the Lean's transfer end to end — measure k ω is the invariant law built from the actual colouring by iterated invariant means and Riesz–Markov; the interface is ProperColoring k c and nothing more; the Lean reaches positivity of the projection by a contraction argument instead of identifying a factor, formalises only the easy half of the spectral claim and never uses Bochner, gets a jointly measurable version correct at every point x rather than almost every x, and calls Theorem 2.3 at exactly one place, residual_fourier → wild_fourier, including the ergodic decomposition — and produced its own rational counterexample: run §4 itself on the proper two-colouring of the rational graph and the measure it manufactures is rotation-invariant, misses the continuous characters and is not Haar (its correlation is ½ or 0 by a parity that is discontinuous everywhere; exact fractions, 2,556 rational units, five controls). The §5–6 seat found where five enters (Proposition 5.6: a third label in a palette leaves the complementary pair, 5 − 3 = 2 — the bound is k − 3, so the argument is tight at five and breaks first there at six), checked the sixty-degree exclusion on the hexagonal 7-colouring (0 of 163,840 angles overlap; an improper colouring overlaps 2.9–4.2%), the expansions of Lemma 5.5 (remainder/d² at 0.70–0.72; a wrong-phase control grows like 1/d), the circle-measure Fourier decay (|J0(r)|(1+r)1/2 bounded, ≈ 1.15) and the five-cycle combinatorics (no solution once Lemma 6.7's bans apply, 40 without them). The §7 seat verified the planar cover obstruction by an exact SAT encoding of covers of discretised domains (UNSAT at multiplicity ≤ 2 and mesh ≤ 2 for disks of inradius 2.5 and 2.13; SAT, as it must be, at multiplicity 3, at mesh 5, and for a thin rectangle), corrected two things in our own brief (the obstruction is the paper's Lemma 7.5 on a square of side ten, and Lemma 4.3's first assertion is on Theorem 1.4's critical path — verified), and recorded that the Lean takes a different route through §7 (an acyclic crossing graph and fixed-radius centre disks instead of Lemmas 7.7–7.8 as written) so the kernel proves the theorem without certifying those paper steps. The §8 seat redid the Moser placement in exact arithmetic over ℚ(√3, √11) — all eleven edges of squared length exactly 1, the graph with no proper three-colouring, all seven vertices strictly inside the region of Lemma 8.8 with the tightest margin at vertex B, P′ − P ≈ 7·10−4 (certified ≥ 6.96·10−4), the paper's own §8.6 residuals reproduced to the digit, seven corruptions rejected — which agrees with our own exact check (the one finite piece we re-derived ourselves, with the region test failing at z = i/2 and z = 1 as controls), enumerated the 34 six-position words of Lemma 8.3, exhausted the five-cycle sign system (0 of 32,768 patterns) and noted that Proposition 8.2 does not actually need the palette bound it cites. The whole-paper seat built the dependency graph, confirmed that nothing in the 70 modules weakens the statement (no instance touches the norm or the measure on ℂ; the Fact instances — 0 < 2π, and on our own grep also 2 ≠ ⊤ in ℝ≥0∞ — are true; WeakColoring is equivalent to Definition 1.2, whose verbatim transcription sits in Basic.lean, which Five.lean does not import; the seven-colouring is formalised separately as Problem160.properColoring_seven), produced two more rational counterexamples by exact arithmetic (Haar measure on the annihilator of the span of the rational units, which excludes 1/3 — the one reasoned out above — and laws built from the parity character, −1 on all 3,820 rational units tested), found that divisibility of K is not the deciding property (roots of unity are divisible and still give a counterexample), stated the general theorem the method proves (a real algebraic field closed under square roots, rotations divisible by 2 and 3), located where the hexagonal seven-colouring escapes the §5–8 chain (§8.4, as it must), and raised one flag that is not an error: Proposition 4.2's proof never uses that the forbidden distance is 1, so read verbatim for any set of real-algebraic distances, together with Furstenberg–Katznelson–Weiss, it would settle Soifer's 2n-distance problem and Kahle's n!-distance problem, which Davies–McCarty–Pilipczuk listed as open in 2023 — the distance-set transfer behind it is one Sergi Galán had already stated publicly on 10 October ("This is OpenAI's transfer, run with several distances and for fractional colourings": for every set D of algebraic distances, χ(ℝ², D) ≥ 1/mD(ℝ²)), without naming Soifer's or Kahle's problems — consistent with everything known, clear of Bukh's conjecture on algebraically independent distances, and a reason to keep the scrutiny on Theorem 2.3. It also caught the trap we had walked into ourselves: the text at (8.6) says "a rotation followed by a translation", the displayed formula is translate-then-rotate, and read literally three Moser vertices fall outside the region — the formula and Table 1 are right (our own check now carries that literal reading as a logged control: it puts exactly those three vertices, 0, uB and uT, outside the region).

Using Minamo Minamoto's recent Q7/Q8 constructions (arXiv:2603.29127) as the immediate baseline, I found explicit C4-free edge-list certificates for Q9 through Q15.

To my knowledge, no explicit C4-free edge-list certificates for Q_n with n >= 9 have previously appeared in the published literature. I would be very happy to be corrected on this point.

A secondary numerical point is that the Q9, Q10, and Q11 certificates slightly improve the commonly cited Brass–Harborth–Nienborg general estimate.

n certificate edges BHN general estimate difference Q_n 4-cycles checked
9 1,505 1,497.6 +7.4 4,608
10 3,304 3,288.6 +15.4 11,520
11 7,164 7,160.3 +3.7 28,160
12 15,372 15,480.5 -108.5 67,584
13 32,856 33,269.8 -413.8 159,744
14 69,909 71,137.2 -1,228.2 372,736
15 148,126 151,434.7 -3,308.7 860,160

Here I use the general estimate

0.5 * (n + 0.9 sqrt(n)) * 2^(n-1)

as cited for general n >= 9 in Minamoto's paper.

Each entry is witnessed by an explicit JSON edge-list certificate. I verified each certificate by exhaustive enumeration of all 4-cycles in the corresponding hypercube; the number of violating 4-cycles is zero in all seven cases.

The repository is here:

https://github.com/rafalwronapl/erdos86-hypercube-c4

It contains:

the seven edge-list certificates,
SHA-256 hashes,
a standalone four-cycle enumeration verifier,
a separate sparse cherry-counting verifier,
a draft note and lift parameters.

The construction uses a two-stage computational pipeline: product lifts via hypercube automorphisms, followed by local ILP repair on C4-incidence neighborhoods. The repair step was essential for the final certificate counts.

The current certificate set, including the improved Q11 = 7164 certificate, was independently checked by Minamo Minamoto using a different method, namely sparse cherry counting. This independent check confirmed the certificate-level claims: matching SHA-256 hashes, the stated edge counts, valid hypercube edges, full vertex support, no loops or duplicates, and no C4s.

This is not a claim about novelty or optimality; it is an independent certificate-level verification of the explicit edge-list witnesses.

These are lower bounds only. I make no exactness claim for any n >= 7. Also, only Q9–Q11 improve the BHN general estimate; Q12–Q15 are included as explicit certificate witnesses and as part of the construction chain, not as improvements over that estimate.

AI disclosure: this work was substantially AI-assisted. I used AI to help design and run the computational search pipeline and to draft parts of the accompanying text. I am posting this primarily as a certificate-verification request: the mathematical claim should be judged by the explicit edge lists and the standalone verifiers, not by authorship.

I would appreciate verification, error reports, and pointers to any prior published or unpublished explicit certificate constructions for these dimensions.

#86: a 1,506-edge C4-free subgraph of Q9

PARTIALAUG 11, 2026

The target (erdosproblems.com/86, OPEN). The n-dimensional hypercube Qn has the binary strings of length n as vertices, with edges between strings differing in one coordinate. If f(n) is the largest number of edges in a C4-free subgraph of Qn, Erdős asked whether

f(n)≤(1/2+o(1))n·2n−1.

We do not resolve that asymptotic question. We improve one explicit finite lower bound:

f(9)≥1,506.

The certificate. The public graph lists 1,506 distinct pairs of vertices in {0,...,511}. Every pair differs in exactly one bit, so every row is an edge of Q9. A four-cycle in a hypercube must alternate two coordinate directions and is therefore one of its square 2-faces. Q9 has

C(9,2)·27=4,608

such faces. Exhaustive verification finds zero faces containing all four boundary edges. A second checker builds adjacency from scratch and examines all 7,426 length-two paths: no unordered endpoint pair has two distinct common neighbours. Either check proves that the graph has no C4.

One edge beyond the identified public frontier. A May 2026 forum post supplied a 1,505-edge Q9 certificate. The fixed current repository version and graph hash are frozen in the verification package. The new graph shares 1,313 edges with it, adds 193, and removes 192, for a net gain of one. The public 1,505-edge graph also passes the independent checker.

Generation and hostile audit. A deterministic min-conflicts search starting from the public graph found the certificate; the heuristic is provenance, not proof. Its one-thread seed-1 rerun reproduces the canonical JSON byte for byte. The hostile audit used a strict clean-room parser, enumerated every face and common-neighbour pair independently, and confirmed that a controlled mutation completing one square is rejected. A decorrelated 12-thread rerun found a different valid 1,506-edge graph as well.

Scope. This proves only f(9)≥1,506. It does not establish f(9) exactly, prove optimality, or move the asymptotic bounds. The live problem page had no claimed proof or current worker, and the current paper, forum, OEIS, repository, and exact-code searches exposed no 1,506-edge construction on 2026-08-11. That supports comparison with the identified public 1,505-edge certificate, but cannot rule out private or unindexed prior work.

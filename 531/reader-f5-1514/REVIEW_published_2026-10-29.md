**Verdict: VERIFIED** (as a certificate: F(5) ≥ 1514). Reviewed 29 October 2026.

**The claim.** F(k) is the least N such that every 2-colouring of {1,…,N} contains a k-element set A whose nonempty subset sums all have one colour. As in your F(4) submission and our July report, the sums must lie in {1,…,N}, i.e. sum(A) ≤ N, and A is a set of distinct positive integers. `witnesses/k5_w1513.txt` (repository nakata-app/erdos-531-folkman at d6009ad, SHA-256 b620e7c0…6cc1) is a 2-colouring of {1,…,1513} with no monochromatic 5-set, so F(5) > 1513, that is, F(5) ≥ 1514. The file is clean: 1,513 characters `0`/`1` and a final newline, nothing else. We ran none of your code.

**Two checks of our own, by different algorithms.**

1. `fs_grow.c` grows monochromatic sets with bitsets. A set A whose subset sums all lie in colour class C extends by x > max A exactly when x lies in C ∩ (C − s) for every subset sum s of A. That condition also forces sum(A) + x ≤ N. Output on the witness: monochromatic 1-, 2-, 3- and 4-sets, by colour, 755/758, 70,979/71,587, 737,148/731,771 and 253,481/239,896; monochromatic 5-sets **0/0**. Runtime 1.5 s.
2. `fs5_direct.c` is plain nested loops a < b < c < d < e over a byte array, with the sum bound in the loop limits. At each depth it tests the new subset sums, the ones containing the newest element, explicitly (1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + 16 = 31), and moves on at the first wrong colour. This is an exhaustive search over all 537,964,268,950 eligible five-sets with sum ≤ 1513 (the count is from an independent partition DP). Output: **0** monochromatic. Runtime 2.8 s.

**Controls** (`controls/`).
- All-zero colourings of [40], [80] and [120]: both checkers count every eligible five-set (2,602, 142,704 and 1,274,795). The direct loops in count-only mode and the partition DP give the same numbers.
- 36 random colourings (12 seeds; [1513] at p = 0.5 and 0.45, [600] at p = 0.3): both checkers give identical monochromatic counts, from 502 to 351,443.
- A third instrument: last week's set-based Python grower (`fs_extend.py`, a different implementation) agrees on the witness cut to [500] (0) and on a random [500] (7; one of them is {2, 60, 75, 135, 208}, found by all three).
- All 1,513 single-bit flips of the witness: both checkers give the same count on every one, 0 disagreements, and so does the referee's separately written census. 1,495 flips create at least one monochromatic 5-set (from 1 to 31, median 7; the most at position 367). 18 positions can be flipped alone without creating one: 25, 1280, 1305, 1321, 1331, 1384, 1443, 1461, 1462, 1469, 1473, 1477, 1484, 1487, 1489, 1502, 1505, 1511.
- 1514 is a wall for this colouring. Colour 0 at 1514 creates exactly one monochromatic set, {82, 285, 348, 372, 427}; colour 1 creates exactly one, {182, 187, 206, 376, 563}. Both sum to 1514.
- Known small values through the grower: the [8] and [53] colourings from our July report have no monochromatic 2-set and 3-set respectively; both extensions of the [53] one to 54 have one; all 512 colourings of [9] contain a monochromatic 2-set (F(2) = 9).

**Hostile referee.** A separately briefed Opus seat with tools, told to refute and kept away from our checkers, returned **holds**. It wrote three checkers of its own (C recursion in descending order, Python big-integer bitsets, and a census of sets that miss by one sum), and all three read 0. It checked the convention against the live statement, Erdős–Spencer ("P(S) ⊂ [n]") and BENTW, and the off-by-one: a clean colouring of [1513] restricts to a clean colouring of every smaller N. It verified two flip-created sets by hand, reading each colour from the file. One, {72, 100, 403, 452, 486}, sums to exactly 1513, the boundary case; the other, {12, 51, 97, 109, 195}, has coinciding subset sums (12 + 97 = 109). It also calibrated its own SAT encoding on F(2) = 9 and F(3) = 54. Its only note concerns the repository README: "flipping one colour of the witness yields 7 monochromatic 5-sets" names no position, and a single flip gives anywhere from 0 to 31.

**Novelty.** We found no value or specific lower bound for F(5) in the literature. Erdős and Spencer (1989), and Balogh, Eberhard, Narayanan, Treglown and Wagner (2017, arXiv:1703.02473), give general lower bounds. BENTW's F(k) ≥ 2^(2^(k−1)/k) gives F(5) ≥ 10, and the trivial argument gives F(5) ≥ 16 (the only 5-set with sum ≤ 15 is {1,…,5}, whose subset sums cover all of [15]). Balogh–Lavrov–Shakan–Wagner (2019) list no small values; Taylor (1981) we could not read (paywall), though both later papers cite it only for the upper bound; OEIS has nothing. On 29 October, erdosproblems.com/531 has no proof claims. Your pull request teorth/erdosproblems#490 (open) records the same bound. So F(5) ≥ 1514 appears to be the first explicit lower bound for k = 5 beyond the general ones. It says nothing about the exact value, and nothing about the growth of F(k), which is what the problem asks.

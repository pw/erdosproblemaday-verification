### 3.2 Is the method new? (public record)
- Ingredients are classical, and experts say so: polynomial/interpolation-determinant method with Frobenius congruences (Laurent 1991; Masser 1981; Fischler 2005; Bombieri–Vaaler; Stepanov; Dobrowolski 1979 — Pratt 19 Oct: "reminiscent of Dobrowolski's arguments"), a baby version "in Disquisitiones, art 129" (Voloch), a similar argument in Selberg 1949 pp. 71–72 with error term too weak (Tao 24 Oct, via Green and Kerr), and Sylvester 1852 for the discriminant fact (Sawin). Tao 19 Oct: "the infamous parity problem blocks [classical analytic] methods ... in this case, said input comes from the polynomial method"; and that a theory of "Zariski complexity" of grids plus Masser's 1981 bound might have led here earlier.
- Novelty = application: no source I found shows anyone previously bounded the *prime mass of chi(p)=-1* by a determinant argument strong enough for Siegel zeros; Speyer (24 Oct): "I've never seen anyone do an error bound about what it would mean for Siegel zeroes, but I assume that someone must have tried this" (no one produced a reference); Tao asks readers for "further historical information on the development of the method".
- Contrast with prior Siegel-zero/class-number work: (i) Siegel/Page/Linnik–Deuring–Heilbronn/Heath-Brown: analytic (zero repulsion, density) and ineffective or conditional on a hypothetical exceptional zero; (ii) Gelfond–Linnik (1948/49, Thue method + Gelfond's 1939 two-logarithm bound) and Baker–Stark (1966–67): transcendence methods, but only effective finiteness for class number 1 and 2 (per Wikipedia Stark–Heegner and Soundararajan arXiv:0707.0237 as retrieved by search), no uniform L(1,chi) lower bound; (iii) Goldfeld 1976 / Gross–Zagier 1986: effective h(d) >> (log d)^(1-eps) via elliptic-curve L-series, far from the logarithmic gap; (iv) Y. Zhang (2007 arXiv:0705.4306; 2022 arXiv:2211.02515): analytic attempt, "(log D)^-2022" type gap, "known to experts to have problems" for the 2007 version (Woit 2022 blog); Tao 20 Oct hopes Zhang's incomplete preprints "now receive some more attention". (v) The new argument bypasses zeros altogether: it bounds sum_{chi(p)=-1} log p/p <= (1/2+eps)log X for X >= q^C by comparing archimedean size and p-adic divisibility of one determinant/Gram integer (Tao: "an adelic polynomial method"; Pollack: "was thought to lie very deep").

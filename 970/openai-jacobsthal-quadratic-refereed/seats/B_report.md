**"Why that count" paragraph (item 1)**
- BFT v3 (1.12): "At present, the sharpest known bounds on W_y are (4+o(1)) y log₂y/log²y ≤ W_y ≤ …, the lower bound
  being a consequence of Iwaniec's theory". The year 2023 matches the bib (Inventiones 233).
- I re-derived the 4 from f(s) = 2e^γ log(s−1)/s at s = 2 + 2 log log y/log y.
- Theorem 1.2's cutoff makes s = log Y/log z ≈ 2 − 4 log log Y/log Y < 2, i.e. past 2, where f = 0.
- The three-times recomputation:
  - Each prime p ∈ ((y/log y)^{1/2}, ~½√y log y] removes at most 2(y/p)/log(y/p) ≈ 4(y/p)/log y survivors (BFT §2.3
    form).
  - Σ1/p over that range ≈ log(log z₂/log z₁) ≈ 3 log log y/log y.
  - The total is ≈ 12 y log log y/(log y)², three times 4 y log log y/(log y)². The page is right.
- Exceptional zeros (BFT Thm 2.2, Thm 9.1, proof of 2.2):
  - With exceptional zeros, liminf W_y/(y/log y) = 0, and the contrapositive is as stated.
  - Thm 9.1 gives W_y ≪ δ_q y = y log q/log²y at log y = (log q/δ_q)^{1/2}.
  - Siegel (δ_q ≫_ε q^{−ε}) gives log log y = ½(log log q + log 1/δ_q) = o(log q), so log q/log log y → ∞.
  - No conflict with c·y log log y/(log y)². Every sentence in that paragraph is true.

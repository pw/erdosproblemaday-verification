What is the size of the largest Sidon subset 𝐴 ⊆{1,22,…,𝑁2}? Is it 𝑁1−𝑜⁡(1)?
#773: [Er80,p.109][AlEr85]
number theory | sidon sets | squares

A question of Alon and Erdős [AlEr85], who proved |𝐴| ≥𝑁2/3−𝑜⁡(1) is possible (via a random subset), and observed that
|𝐴|≪
𝑁
(log⁡𝑁)1/4
,
since (as shown by Landau) the density of the sums of two squares decays like (log⁡𝑁)−1/2. This upper bound was improved to
|𝐴|≤𝑁1−
𝑐
log⁡log⁡𝑁
for some constant 𝑐 >0 by Croot, Mao, and Yip (see the comments).

The lower bound was improved to
|𝐴|≫𝑁2/3
by Lefmann and Thiele [LeTh95].

In [Er80] Erdős further defines 𝑔⁡(𝐴) to be the maximal size of a Sidon set of {𝑎2 :𝑎 ∈𝐴}, and asks whether 𝑔⁡(𝐴) ≥𝑔⁡({1,…,𝑁}) where 𝑁 =|𝐴|. (See [530] for the linear analogue.)

He further asks the infinite analogue: is there an infinite set 𝐴 ⊂ℕ such that |𝐴 ∩[1,𝑁]| ≥𝑁1−𝑜⁡(1) for all large 𝑁 and {𝑎2 :𝑎 ∈𝐴} is a Sidon set?

See [1206] for a similar question for higher powers.

What is the size of the largest Sidon subset 𝐴 ⊆{1,22,…,𝑁2}
? Is it 𝑁1−𝑜⁡(1)
?
#773: [Er80,p.109][AlEr85]
number theory | sidon sets | squares
The open status of this problem reflects the current belief of the owner of this website. A question of Alon and Erdős [AlEr85], who proved |𝐴| ≥𝑁2/3−𝑜⁡(1)
 is possible (via a random subset), and observed that
|𝐴|≪
𝑁
(log⁡𝑁)1/4
,
since (as shown by Landau) the density of the sums of two squares decays like (log⁡𝑁)−1/2
. This upper bound was improved to
|𝐴|≤𝑁1−
𝑐
log⁡log⁡𝑁
for some constant 𝑐 >0
 by Croot, Mao, and Yip (see the comments).

The lower bound was improved to
|𝐴|≫𝑁2/3
by Lefmann and Thiele [LeTh95].

In [Er80] Erdős further defines 𝑔⁡(𝐴)
 to be the maximal size of a Sidon set of {𝑎2 :𝑎 ∈𝐴}
, and asks whether 𝑔⁡(𝐴) ≥𝑔⁡({1,…,𝑁})
 where 𝑁 =|𝐴|
. (See [530] for the linear analogue.)

He further asks the infinite analogue: is there an infinite set 𝐴 ⊂ℕ
 such that |𝐴 ∩[1,𝑁]| ≥𝑁1−𝑜⁡(1)
 for all large 𝑁
 and {𝑎2 :𝑎 ∈𝐴}
 is a Sidon set?

See [1206] for a similar question for higher powers.

My student Junzhe Mao and postdoc Kyle Yip had worked on this problem some, and then Junzhe made an advance on it, and we have a "proof from the book" upper bound.  This and other results will be included in a forthcoming paper we are writing. Just thought I would mention one more thing. See this paper (Theorem 1.2):

https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/rsa.20496

If the squares in [𝑁2]
 behave like random sets of size 𝑁
, then one would expect that the largest Sidon set contained within it has size about 𝑁2/3+𝑜⁡(1)
. If so, then it means that improving the lower bound by finding a better construction is a fool's errand; and that what one *should* be doing is trying to improve the upper bound.

I'd personally put 20% effort on improving the lower bound and 80% on the upper bound.

It's possible that the squares may "play nice" with some magic algebraic construction, but I'm pessimistic. The random model in that paper, by the way, does take into account magic sets like this, since it's the outer sets that are randomly chosen, and then inner set that gives the largest Sidon set is the one whose size it tries to predict -- and this one matches algebraic constructions.

It would interesting to try to come up with some nice algebraic or interesting quasi-algebraic set where the largest Sidon set inside it is larger than the random model would predict for a set of similar density -- in case the lower bound on #773 can be improved, yet the improvement is very hard to find. e.g. maybe something like the set of numbers that are the sum of two 4th powers, which seems like it might be close enough to being Sidon that all you need is a small tweak.

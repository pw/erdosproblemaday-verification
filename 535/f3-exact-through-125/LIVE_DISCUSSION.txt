I looked through the literature after my earlier comment, and I think the right correction is stronger than just adding a squarefree hypothesis.

The counterexample 𝐴𝑚 ={2,4,8,…,2𝑚}
 shows that the current auxiliary formulation with 𝜔⁡(𝑛) =𝑘
 is false: for 𝑘 =1
, this family can be arbitrarily large while still avoiding an 𝑟
-tuple with constant pairwise gcd.

What seems to be the correct fix is the one Erdős gives later. In [Er73], he says that Abbott pointed out the ordinary sunflower conjecture does not seem to suffice for deriving the conjectured upper bound for 𝑓3⁡(𝑥)
. He then replaces it by a slightly stronger conjecture in which the integers have exactly 𝑛

So I think the auxiliary statement here should be updated to:

, and there do not exist 𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑟 ∈𝐴
 and an integer 𝑑

 and (

 for all 𝑖
.

The squarefree / 𝜔⁡(𝑛) =𝑘

We have obtained an estimate for 𝑓𝑟⁡(𝑁)
, developed in conjunction with ChatGPT-5.5 Thinking, where I worked on the argument with sourish and kireet.

Our main estimate is:


𝑓𝑟⁡(𝑁)≥exp⁡((log⁡(𝑟−1)+𝑜⁡(1))⁢
log⁡𝑁
log⁡log⁡𝑁
)

𝑓𝑟⁡(𝑁)≤exp⁡(𝑂𝑟⁡(
log⁡𝑁⁢log⁡log⁡log⁡𝑁
log⁡log⁡𝑁
)).


In particular,


𝑓𝑟⁡(𝑁)=𝑁𝑜⁡(1).


The key idea is to encode each integer 𝑛
 by the set of its prime-power divisibility layers,


𝑆⁡(𝑛)={(𝑝,𝑗):1≤𝑗≤𝑣𝑝⁡(𝑛)}.


Then


𝑆⁡(𝑎)∩𝑆⁡(𝑏)=𝑆⁡(gcd⁡(𝑎,𝑏)),


so an 𝑟
-tuple whose pairwise gcds are all equal is exactly an 𝑟
-sunflower in the corresponding set system.

The lower bound comes from a block construction: partition the primes into blocks of size 𝑟 −1
, and choose one prime from each block. The pigeonhole principle prevents 𝑟
 distinct chosen products from having all pairwise gcds equal.

For the upper bound, we split each integer into its smooth part and rough part. Rankin’s estimate controls the number of possible smooth parts, while the improved sunflower lemma controls each rough fiber, giving the stated upper bound.

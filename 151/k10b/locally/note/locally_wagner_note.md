# There are exactly two connected locally Wagner graphs

*Draft v2, 2026-10-12 (Claude; staged for Patrick — authorship, venue and the AI-assistance disclosure arXiv requires are his call). Everything below is reproducible from `verification/151/k10b/locally/` in the ErdosProblemADay repository (github.com/pw/ErdosProblemADay.com). A blind cold-read of v1 is in `note/coldread_note_opus_2026-10-12.md`; its findings are applied here.*

**Abstract.** A graph is *locally X* if the neighbourhood of every vertex induces a graph isomorphic to X. Let M₈ be the Wagner graph (the Möbius ladder on 8 vertices, the circulant C₈(1,4)). We show, by an exhaustive search carried out by two independent implementations, that the connected locally-M₈ graphs are exactly two: the Paley graph P(17), and the graph of *opposite flags* of the Fano plane on 21 vertices (the 21 flags (p, L) of PG(2,2), two flags adjacent when p ∉ M and q ∉ L). The second graph is a counterexample to Theorem 4.17 of C. W. Smallwood's 2019 thesis, which states that P(17) is the only M₈-local graph. Neither graph arrows (K₃, K₃) in edges. The question arose in the Erdős–Gallai clique-transversal problem (erdosproblems.com #151): there, a K₄-free minimum counterexample at the first open value H(n) = 10 would contain a minimal (3,3)-Ramsey graph on 21 to 41 vertices which, if 8-regular, must be locally Wagner.

## 1. Statement

**Theorem (computer-assisted).** A connected graph in which every vertex neighbourhood induces the Wagner graph M₈ is isomorphic to P(17) or to the graph F₂₁ of opposite flags of PG(2,2) described below. There are no others, of any order.

The proof is a finite computation (§3): a completion search that, started from one vertex and its link, closes or kills every branch with at most 23 vertices in play, and run with a vertex cap of 1000 refuses no branch — so no bound on the order is assumed.

## 2. The two graphs

**P(17).** The Paley graph on 𝔽₁₇, x ~ y iff x − y is a nonzero square. It is the (4,4;17) Ramsey graph: strongly regular with parameters (17, 8, 3, 4), self-complementary, K₄-free, α = 3, |Aut| = 136. Every link is a 3-regular triangle-free graph on 8 vertices with independence number 3, hence M₈ (the only other cubic triangle-free graph on 8 vertices is the cube, with α = 4). 68 edges, 68 triangles.

**F₂₁.** Let PG(2,2) be the Fano plane (points 0..6, lines {i, i+1, i+3} mod 7). Vertices: the 21 flags (p, L) with p ∈ L. Adjacency: (p, L) ~ (q, M) iff p ∉ M and q ∉ L ("opposite" flags: neither point lies on the other line). Facts (computer-verified, `models_g21.py`, `test_found.py`, `invariants.py`, and independently by the cold-reader): 8-regular, 84 edges, 84 triangles, every link ≅ M₈, K₄-free, α = 5, girth 3, diameter 2; a non-adjacent pair has 2 common neighbours (84 pairs; they form an edge) or 4 (42 pairs; they form a 4-cycle), so F₂₁ is not strongly regular; vertex-transitive with |Aut| = 336 and no automorphism of order 21, so not a circulant; Aut(F₂₁) ≅ PGL(2,7) — the 168 collineations of PG(2,2) together with the 168 correlations act on flags and exhaust the group (element-order census matches; also confirmed in GAP). Equivalent descriptions: the distance-3 graph of the line graph of the Heawood graph (the usual *flag graph* of PG(2,2) is L(Heawood), two flags adjacent when they share a point or a line; F₂₁ joins the flags at distance 3 in it); a Cayley graph of the Frobenius group ℤ₇ ⋊ ℤ₃. graph6: `T|fLKfHWAXkoOLKsEAorDEEWKCwKQWEKk@zB`.

The degree by hand: for a flag (p, L), the neighbours are the flags (q, M) with q one of the 4 points off L and M one of the 2 lines through q missing p (3 lines through q, one of which is qp) — 8 neighbours. That these 8 induce M₈ is checked by computer for all 21 flags (one hand case suffices by transitivity, but we have not written it out).

## 3. The classification

**Method** (`locally.py`). Vertex 0 receives a copy of X as its neighbourhood. Vertices are then completed in index order: for vertex v, the set K of already-known neighbours must embed into X respecting every *decided* pair (an existing edge must go to an edge of X, a forced non-edge to a non-edge; undecided pairs are free and are decided by the embedding); embeddings are taken up to Aut(X) (one representative per orbit, the lexicographically least image tuple); the remaining positions of X are filled by existing vertices whose adjacency to v is still undecided, or by new vertices (new vertices are interchangeable, so they are allocated in position order and tried once); the edges and non-edges among N(v) are then forced from X and v is closed, every undecided pair at v becoming a non-edge. A branch dies on an edge into a closed vertex or across a forced non-edge, a forced non-edge across an existing edge, a vertex of degree exceeding |X|, or (optionally) a vertex cap. Completeness. Let G be a connected locally-X graph and fix v₀. Run the search choosing, at every branch, the option that agrees with G: vertex 0 = v₀ and N(v₀) labelled through an isomorphism N(v₀) → X (the quotient by Aut(X) costs nothing here: if φ′ = σ∘φ, the completions through φ′ are the completions through φ with the slot labels permuted by σ, so the same labelled graphs arise); when vertex v is completed, G's own link isomorphism restricted to the already-labelled neighbours K is an admissible embedding (it respects every decided pair, since decided pairs are pairs of G), the orbit representative of it is admissible too, the labelled vertices adjacent to v in G are chosen for their slots and the remaining neighbours of v become new vertices; closing v is right because N(v) is then complete. By induction the labelled partial graph is always isomorphic to the induced subgraph of G on the labelled vertices, and since G is connected and every labelled vertex is eventually completed, every vertex of G is labelled; the branch ends at a graph isomorphic to G.

**Result.** For X = M₈ the search closes after 17,950 nodes (30–40 s) with the two graphs above; the largest partial graph it ever holds has 23 vertices and, run with caps 41 and 1000, it refuses no branch — so the theorem holds without any bound on the order.

**Controls.** Classical answers reproduced exactly by the same code: locally K₃ → K₄; locally C₄ → octahedron; locally C₅ → icosahedron; locally Petersen, order ≤ 21 → the Kneser graph K(7,2) alone (Hall 1980: the locally Petersen graphs are three, on 21, 63 and 65 vertices; the two larger ones were not reproduced, neither program finished that case). Locally C₆, order ≤ 16, gives eleven graphs — not a literature value, but a case with several answers, and the two programs agree on it (and on 25 graphs up to order 20, of which the 18 toroidal ones match a lattice construction). A first version of the program failed the C₄ and C₅ controls — it treated an undecided pair as a non-edge in the embedding test — and was corrected before any M₈ run.

**Second implementation and attacks** (a separately briefed hostile reviewer with tools; report in the bundle). An independently written search (different vertex order, its own automorphism and isomorphism routines, nauty canonical forms for the output) returns the same two graphs with at most 21 vertices in play and no branch refused at caps 41, 60 and 1000. Three deliberate corruptions of `locally.py` (a non-automorphism admitted to the quotient; never reusing an existing vertex; dropping one orbit representative) each lose a graph on M₈ and on a locally-cube control, so the controls can see these failures. Further controls on which both programs agree: locally Q₃ → the 24-cell graph and the complement of K₃ □ K₅ (Buset 1983); locally 3×3-rook → the complement of K₄ □ K₄ and J(6,3); locally K₃,₃ → K₃,₃,₃ (these two as listed in Smallwood's survey chapter and in Hall 1985 for links on ≤ 6 vertices — we have not read the primary sources). Locally icosahedron up to 40 vertices was finished only by the second program (the 40-vertex quotient of the 600-cell). Independent cross-checks: among all Cayley graphs of all 168 groups of order 9–41 (GAP), a locally-M₈ connection set exists only for ℤ₁₇ (P(17)), ℤ₇ ⋊ ℤ₃ (F₂₁) and two groups of order 34 (2·P(17)); and neither graph has a connected non-trivial locally-M₈ covering graph, since both triangle complexes are simply connected.

## 4. Neither graph arrows (K₃, K₃)

Each has a 2-colouring of its edges with no monochromatic triangle (found by a SAT solver and by CP-SAT, re-verified by a backtracking colourer, and recounted literally: 0 monochromatic triangles out of 84 resp. 68; the red edge lists are `G21.colouring`, `P17.colouring`). This is what the question was for. Erdős and Gallai asked whether τ(G) ≤ n − H(n) for every graph G on n vertices, where τ is the clique-transversal number and H(n) = max{k : R(3,k) ≤ n}; the first open case is H(n) = 10, n ∈ {40, 41}. There, every counterexample arrows (K₃,K₃), and for a K₄-free minimum counterexample the minimal (3,3)-Ramsey subgraph has every degree 8 or 9 (Bikov 2016, Thm 8.2: δ ≥ 8 with seven admissible degree-8 links), at least 21 vertices (Bikov–Nenov 2020, F_e(3,3;4) ≥ 21) and at most 41 (Angeltveit 2025, R(3,10) ≤ 41); Seymour's bound on minimally non-2-colourable hypergraphs applied to its triangle hypergraph, together with a bound of three on the "exceptional" vertices that holds in that setting, forces an 8-regular such subgraph to be locally M₈ at every vertex (erdosproblemaday.com/day/151-k10-the-core-is-locally-wagner, bundle `verification/151/k10b`). The theorem leaves F₂₁ as the only candidate, and F₂₁ does not arrow — so that subgraph has exactly two vertices of degree 9.

## 5. On Smallwood (2019)

C. W. Smallwood, *Classification of graphs that are locally Mobius ladder*, M.S. thesis, University of Alabama in Huntsville, 2019 (louis.uah.edu/uah-theses/296). The thesis takes graphs finite and connected and the local graph to be the induced neighbourhood, as here. Its Theorem 4.17 ends "Thus, it has been shown that (ℤ₁₇, Q) is the only M₈-local graph." F₂₁ is a counterexample. In the thesis's case analysis, the distance-2 profile (p₂, p₃, c₄) of F₂₁ — from a vertex, 8 non-neighbours with common neighbourhood K₂ and 4 with common neighbourhood C₄ — is exactly its "Case 1: (8, 0, 4)", which the proof claims leads to a contradiction (an induced P₄ inside a common neighbourhood); F₂₁ realises that case. (Its Corollary 4.7 also omits C₄ from the list of possible common neighbourhoods, although the paragraph before Lemma 4.5 includes it.) MathWorld's "Local Graph" table lists only P(17) as an example for the Wagner graph (it makes no uniqueness claim). We read the thesis from a Wayback Machine copy; the university's PDF server rejects automated access. We found no other source classifying locally-M₈ graphs and none naming F₂₁; the nearest relative in the literature is the Coxeter graph on the 28 antiflags of PG(2,2).

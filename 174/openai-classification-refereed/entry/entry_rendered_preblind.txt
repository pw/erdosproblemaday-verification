  ERDŐS #174

  #174: an exact algebraic test for which finite point sets are Euclidean Ramsey — OpenAI's classification, read by AI seats here and its Lean replayed; Formal Conjectures' open-tagged Graham conjecture refuted in Lean from OpenAI's twelve points; and an eight-point square-plus-rectangle that fails the test while no derivation certificate can see it

  CLOSEDOCT 21, 2026

  Community database OPENas of 2025-08-31tags [/board?tag=geometry] geometry, [/board?tag=ramsey%20theory] ramsey theory[/problem/174] problem page &middot; [https://www.erdosproblems.com/174] erdosproblems.com/174

  Newer public claim (6 Oct 2026): OpenAI's internal-model release claims, for this problem, a full classification of Euclidean Ramsey sets — refereed here 21 Oct, see /day/174-openai-euclidean-ramsey-classification-refereed — family 172 at [https://github.com/openai/math] github.com/openai/math, OpenAI's own Lean proves Theorem 1.1 (A is Ramsey iff the tensor criterion over F ⊗_ℚ F holds) and its consequences (Ramsey sets are cospherical; subsets of finite transitive sets are Ramsey; at most five circle points; quadratic independence; nine generic circle points are not Ramsey), and, in a separate development that does not use the classification, the twelve-point set with a 50-colour obstruction in every dimension. Kernel-replayed on our machine on 21 Oct 2026: 65 modules, 0 errors, standard axioms, all seven statements and their definitions matched to the comparator challenges under pp.all. Kernel-checked bridges of ours refute Formal Conjectures' erdos_174.variants.graham (tagged open there) and prove its variants.spherical — see our /day entry. Bloom calls these claims, not endorsements.

The problem. [https://www.erdosproblems.com/174] Erdős problem #174 [Er75f, p. 108; ErGr79; ErGr80; Er83c]: "A finite set A ⊂ ℝn is called Ramsey if, for any k ≥ 1, there exists some d = d(A,k) such that in any k-colouring of ℝd there exists a monochromatic copy of A. Characterise the Ramsey sets in ℝn." A copy means a congruent copy, at the original scale, and the colourings are arbitrary functions. The page was last edited in October 2025 and, like the rest of the site, no longer shows a status; the teorth database still lists #174 as open. The page records two competing conjectures: Graham's, that every spherical set is Ramsey, and Leader, Russell and Walters' (LRW), that a set is Ramsey exactly when it is a subset of a finite transitive set. Our 11 August entry, [/day/174-ramsey-pyramid-closure] #174: every Ramsey base admits a Ramsey pyramid, covered Moore's one-point extension theorem.

Graham's conjecture was refuted first, by Pálvölgyi. Pálvölgyi's arXiv preprint of 20 September (2609.23327, v1, a manuscript he says was written entirely by ChatGPT) takes r transcendental, r > 2, and the seven points (0,0) and (j, ±√(j(2r−j))) for j = 1, 3, 4. These lie on the circle (x−r)² + y² = r², and Theorem 1 says they are not Ramsey. The proof takes a derivation D of ℝ with D(r) = 1 and integer weights λ whose moments Σλ, Σλp, Σλppᵀ and Σλ·D(p)pᵀ vanish while Σλ‖D(p)‖² does not. The function F(q) = R·Σ D(qi)² then has a fixed weighted sum over every congruent copy in every dimension, and a Rado colouring of its values avoids the set. He posted it on the #174 forum on 23 September.

The claim. OpenAI's family 172, "A classification of finite Euclidean Ramsey configurations" (23 September, 26 pp., released 6 October). Let A = {a1,…,as} ⊂ ℝd, s ≥ 2, with affine span ℝd. Put pi = (1, ai), let F be the field generated over ℚ by all the coordinates, B = F ⊗ℚ F, and mF : B → F the multiplication map. Theorem 1.1: A is Ramsey if and only if there is a (d+1)×(d+1) matrix P over B with (pi ⊗ 1)ᵀ P (1 ⊗ pi) = 0 for every i and mF(Pαβ) = δαβ on the spatial block. Applying mF to the first condition gives an ordinary sphere through the points. The test asks whether that sphere equation lifts to the tensor ring before multiplying. Section 7 derives these consequences:

every Ramsey set is spherical (Cor. 7.1, the 1973 theorem);

every subset of a finite transitive set is Ramsey (Cor. 7.2, the direction of LRW's conjecture that Pálvölgyi announced and has not posted);

spherical sets whose quadratic evaluation rows are independent over F are Ramsey (Prop. 7.3);

every set of at most five points on a circle is Ramsey (Cor. 7.4);

for transcendental a ∈ (−1,1), the kites (−1,0), (1,0), (a, ±√(1−a²)) are Ramsey, while LRW 2011 proved them not subtransitive, so LRW's characterisation fails (Cor. 7.5);

nine points (1−ti², 2ti)/(1+ti²) with t1,…,t9 algebraically independent are not Ramsey (Prop. 7.6);

three concentric squares, the coordinate square and its rotations by ±θ with tan(θ/2) = Liouville's constant, form twelve points that are not Ramsey (Prop. 7.7).

The argument. Necessity (§2): if no such matrix exists, a ℚ-linear functional separates the identity from the image of the evaluation map. Its components give functions hi on ℝ with Σi Hi(bi) = −1 on every congruent copy (bi) in every dimension, where Hi(z) = Σj hi(zj), and Σi Hi = 0 at every single point. Colour z by where each Hi(z) mod 2 falls among 2s+1 intervals. A monochromatic copy would make −1 equal to an even integer plus an error of size less than 1, which is impossible. That is (2s+1)s colours in every dimension. Sufficiency (§§3–6) is the new part. A finite lattice identity (Lemma 3.1, through completion and flatness) and Gaussian smoothing with exact rationalisation (Lemmas 4.2, 4.3) turn the tensor into two lists of affine functions. Evaluated at A, these give two copies of A, at scale 1 and at an arbitrarily small scale q, whose corresponding points have the same coordinates in different orders (Lemma 4.1). Paths in a free group record products of scaled copies (§5). Averaging over coordinate permutations makes the two endpoints agree at small extra cost, through a derived-series argument on right fractions (§6). An ultrafilter realisation and the Hales–Jewett theorem then produce the copy (§5.3). One endpoint is prepared with paths of weights 1, ½, …, 1/n before the colouring is seen. Whatever the number t of variable positions in the monochromatic line, the copy then has squared-distance factor t·(1/t) = 1 exactly, with no continuity needed.

The Lean, and our replay. OpenAI's Lean for this family has two parts that share no imports. OAI/Combinatorics/EuclideanRamsey (47 modules) proves the classification and the consequences. OAI/Combinatorics/SphericalRamsey (18 modules in the closure) proves the twelve-point result directly, without the classification: a derivation D of ℝ with D(t) = (1+t²)/2, the energy Σ D(yj)², weights 1, 1, −2 on the three squares, a weighted sum of 8 on every copy, and the colouring ⌊3e⌋ mod 50. That is stronger than the paper, which states no colour count. We compiled the 65-module closure (9,589 lines; openai/math @ 1581d4c, Lean v4.34.1, Mathlib d13f23b) lake-free on our machine: 65/65 modules, 0 errors, 6 min 7 s wall, peak 6.7 GiB. With --trust=0 the seven comparator theorems depend only on propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. Their statements and definitions, 49 blocks in all, match the seven comparator files under pp.all, with sorryAx visible on every challenge. The matcher passed a mutation test seven ways, and a planted sorry surfaces as sorryAx. Seat L read the source: no sorry, axiom, native_decide, extern, unsafe, set_option or notation tricks, and no instance on any type that appears in a statement. The Lean follows the paper's route stage by stage. Four sub-lemmas (Prop 2.1's descent, Lemma 3.1, Lemma 4.2, Lemma 5.2) are proved there by different arguments, so the kernel checks their statements, not the paper's own proofs of them. Seats O and T read those proofs by hand. Not formalised: Remarks 2.2–2.3, and the non-subtransitivity half of Cor. 7.5, which is a citation to LRW. An independent kernel re-check (leanchecker --fresh) over the whole closure plus our algebraic-coordinates bridge was still running when this page went up; this sentence is updated when it lands.

The reading. Four seats, all Claude Opus 5.5, were briefed to refute, to quote the sentence they objected to, and to grade each objection. Seat O took §§1–4, seat T §§5–6 (the sufficiency half), seat L the Lean against the paper, and seat W the sources and the world. A fifth, blind seat checked this page against the files before it went up. All four returned HOLDS AS READ, with exposition notes only. The main seat also read all of §§2–7 line by line and found nothing to add. O ran Lemma 3.1 through Lemma 4.1 end to end in exact arithmetic on the two-point set {0, 1}: it got ‖f1−f2‖² = 1 and ‖g1−g2‖² = 1/21 with equal coordinate multisets. It also made the necessity functional explicit for non-spherical sets, where it is the classical 1973 invariant, and searched 769,470 copies for a monochromatic one without finding any; with K = 2 or 3 the same search does find them. T wrote out the ultrafilter realisation for a two-point set (30 realisations) and an equilateral triangle (27), each giving distances exactly equal to A's. It built Lemma 6.2's commutator from real unit triangles and checked Lemma 6.3's averaging with Fox derivatives. Its notes: Lemma 5.4's Zorn step does not say the intersection is again a compact left ideal; the citation for the product rule (20) points to the wrong Hindman–Strauss results; (27) should say "for every x ∈ Z"; and in Lemma 6.1 the differences are generated by telescoping, without a normal closure. We recomputed Prop 7.6's determinant exactly (64 at the paper's grid, nonzero at random rational points, zero on the diagonal) and the moment identities (31) of Prop 7.7.

Formal Conjectures, and the bridges (ours). Formal Conjectures (FC) added ErdosProblems/174.lean on 20 October (PR #7469). It tags erdos_174.variants.graham (every finite spherical set is Ramsey) and the LRW equivalence "research open", and it leaves erdos_174.variants.spherical, tagged solved, as a sorry. Our Bridge174FC.lean copies FC's IsRamsey and both statements verbatim, checked by a script whose mutated run fails. It derives the negation of FC's graham statement from OpenAI's twelve-point theorem OAI.GrahamSpherical.full_main. The step that needs care is that a colouring of ℝ0 cannot hold a copy of two distinct points, so FC's "some d" becomes OpenAI's "some d > 0". It also proves FC's spherical statement from OpenAI's ramsey_cospherical, by enumerating the finite set and translating between FC's set-based copies and OpenAI's indexed ones. Both depend only on the three standard axioms. Three mutants fail to compile: the refutation restricted to sets of at most five points, the conclusion strengthened to "A has at most one point", and a changed IsRamsey. A separate leanchecker --fresh over this bridge's closure was also still running; this sentence is updated when it lands. We did not bridge the LRW equivalence. Its "only if" direction is false (Cor. 7.5), but proving that in Lean needs LRW's non-subtransitivity theorem, which we did not find formalised anywhere. An FC issue is drafted and waiting for Patrick, not filed.

Algebraic coordinates (not ours). One consequence follows in a few lines and is in neither the paper nor OpenAI's Lean. If every coordinate is algebraic, F is a number field. Then F ⊗ F splits as a product of fields, the kernel of mF is generated by an idempotent e, and (1−e)·(H ⊗ 1) satisfies the test, with H the ordinary sphere matrix. So every spherical set with algebraic coordinates is Ramsey, and only transcendental coordinates can break Graham's conjecture. This is consistent with every non-Ramsey example above. We derived it, seat L derived it independently, and we kernel-checked it from OpenAI's classification (Bridge174.lean, standard axioms, three mutants fail). Seat W then found the same statement already public, in four AI-generated repositories built on OpenAI's paper. One is JD-Jones-ASES/six-concyclic-lean, from 7 October; its Lean theorem even has the same name as ours, algebraic_spherical_fieldCriterion. The others are Rogerhu12/openai-math-extensions (7 October, TeX), atimics/math2 (8 October, end to end in Lean) and wstrinz/spherical-ramsey (14 October). None has had human review, and W found the statement in no arXiv or peer-reviewed source. Ours is an independent replication, not a first.

Deciding the test with one transcendental (ours; computation, label (d)). The paper notes the test is a finite computation for exact coordinates but gives no procedure (Remark 2.2). When F = ℚ(τ) for one transcendental τ, B embeds in ℚ(x, y), mF is y ↦ x, and B localised at ker mF is a discrete valuation ring with uniformiser x − y. The test then reduces to linear algebra. We compute the kernel of the evaluation equations over ℚ(x, y) and saturate it at x − y. The test holds exactly when some combination of the reduced basis, at y = x, has spatial block I. A saturated basis spans the whole kernel over that ring, so a "fails" is a proof of failure, given Theorem 1.1. A second, separately written instrument uses exact rational arithmetic and no computer algebra: it takes the limit of the kernel as y → x through its Plücker coordinates, the complementary maximal minors interpolated exactly in h = y − x. The controls behave:

the union of two squares (a transitive set) passes;

a transcendental kite passes, as does a five-point set (Cor. 7.4);

three collinear points fail;

seven rational points on a circle pass.

What it found:

Prop 7.7's twelve points fail for a simpler reason than the paper gives. Their 12 × 9 tensor matrix has full rank 9 over ℚ(x, y), checked a second way by exact elimination at five random rational points. So the only P is zero, exactly as in Prop 7.6's determinant argument. Every one of the 220 nine-point subsets is already full rank, so each is non-Ramsey too.

Every subset of the twelve points with at most seven points passes (128 orbits of six-point subsets and 104 of seven-point subsets, under the set's eight symmetries). At eight points, 67 orbits pass and 5 fail. The two instruments agree on every one of the 304 orbits at six, seven and eight points. So the smallest non-Ramsey subsets of OpenAI's twelve points have eight points.

One of the five is the square {(±1,0),(0,±1)} together with the rectangle {(±u,±v)} inscribed in the same circle, with u = cos θ, v = sin θ and tan(θ/2) any transcendental number. Each half is transitive, but the union is not Ramsey (by Theorem 1.1's necessity half and our computation). For this set the moment identities that Pálvölgyi's and Prop 7.7's derivation certificates need have only the zero solution, so no first-order derivation certificate exists. The test still fails, with a reduced kernel whose spatial block is antisymmetric and therefore never the identity. The classification's colouring, a ℚ-linear functional, sees an obstruction that the derivation method cannot. We have not seen this eight-point example stated elsewhere.

Random one-parameter configurations of six and seven points on a circle (Möbius images of τ mixed with rational points) pass: all 40 of the six-point configurations we drew and all 40 of the seven-point ones. This fits JD-Jones' claim that six concyclic points always pass, and Pálvölgyi's heptagon, whose coordinate field ℚ(r, √(2r−1), √(6r−9), √(2r−4)) is not of the form ℚ(τ), is no counterexample to it. Whether every seven-point concyclic set over ℚ(τ) is Ramsey is a question this evidence raises but does not settle.

What this entry is. A referee report on someone else's theorem. The classification, its proof and its Lean are OpenAI's. Graham's conjecture was refuted first by Pálvölgyi, and the algebraic-coordinates corollary was first stated in the four repositories named above. Ours: the replay and its controls, the two bridges to Formal Conjectures with their controls, the readings, and the decision procedure with what it found. In our labels: Theorem 1.1 and its consequences are (d) computer-assisted (a Lean proof, kernel-replayed here), with the paper's own proofs of Lemmas 3.1 and 4.2 read by hand as (b). The bridges are (d). The twelve-point rank observation, the eight-point minimum, the square-plus-rectangle example and its missing derivation certificate are (d) computations whose consequence for Ramsey sets is (b), rigorous modulo Theorem 1.1. The seven-point evidence is (c). If you are looking for the hole, look first at the decision procedure: it rests on the reduction to a discrete valuation ring and on the saturation step, written up with both instruments in the bundle. Then look at §6's cost bookkeeping, the most intricate part of the proof that is not in a standard library.

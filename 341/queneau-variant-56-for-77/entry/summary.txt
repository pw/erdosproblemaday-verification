Erdős–Graham's #341 extends a finite seed by the least integer that is not a sum of two earlier terms, equal terms allowed, and asks whether the gaps are eventually periodic; Zhiheng Li's August seed answered no (refereed here on 13 October). The comments to Problem 7 of Green's list pose the same question for Queneau's variant, where the two earlier terms must be distinct, and mark both open. Li's seed is periodic under that variant. We show the variant fails too: replacing 77 by 56 in his seed — A = {1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 13, 22, 27, 28, 32, 36, 40, 47, 48, 52, 56, 63, 71, 81, 89, 97} — gives a greedy extension whose gaps are not eventually periodic under Queneau's rule, and not under Dickson's either (a 21-element seed outside Li's infinite family). The proof is Li's shield mod 49 with a new controller whose three rails are switched by the blocks [8^k+1, 2·8^k], [4·8^k, 8·8^k], [2·8^k, 4·8^k], every identity checked by hand; the controller was found by letting the greedy itself run on all 27 seeds of this shape. An independent greedy on the integers matches the closed form at every integer to 10^7.

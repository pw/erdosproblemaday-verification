 Related reference: [Conlon, David, and Jacob Fox. "Lines in Euclidean Ramsey theory." Discrete & Computational Geometry 61.1 (2019): 218-225, https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.02166v4]. They study a generalization of this question in $\mathbb{R}^n$, and their Theorem 1.1, applied with $n=2$, yields the bound $k \le 2.15\cdot 10^9$, even though it is likely that one can optimize their proof to deduce a much better upper bound. In the last paragraph of Section 3, they mention another related open problem of Erdos, which is currently not on this website.

 The 2017 paper "A Euclidean Ramsey result in the plane" of Tsaturian shows $k\geq 6$. (Note that Lemma 2.2 should say side length $3$ instead of $\sqrt{3}$.) Additionally, Theorem 1' in the 1973 paper "Euclidean Ramsey Theorems II" by Erdos-Graham-Montgomery-Rothschild-Spencer-Straus already showed that $k\geq 5$, i.e. any such two-coloring contains either a unit-distance pair of red points or a unit distance 4-AP of blue points. The work of [Ju79] showed that one can replace "unit distance 4-AP of blue points" by "congruent copy of any desired planar 4-point configuration. Also relatedly, (G. Currier, K. Moore & C. H. Yip, "Any two-coloring of the plane contains monochromatic 3-term arithmetic progressions", Combinatorica 44 (2024), 1367–1380) shows that any two-coloring of the plane contains a monochromatic unit length 3-AP.

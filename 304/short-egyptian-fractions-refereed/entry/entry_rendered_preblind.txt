      "Let N(b) be the largest number of distinct unit fractions, all with denominators at least 2, that some a/b with 1 ≤ a The problems. Erdős problem #304 [ErGr80, p. 37]: "For integers 1 ≤ a < b let N(a,b) denote the minimal k such that there exist integers 1 < n1 < ⋯ < nk with a/b = 1/n1 + ⋯ + 1/nk. Estimate N(b) = max1≤a<b N(a,b). Is it true that N(b) ≪ log log b?" Erdős [Er50c] proved log log b ≪ N(b) ≪ log b/log log b, the lower bound already for a = b − 1, and Vose [Vo85] improved the upper bound to N(b) ≪ √(log b). Two neighbours share the paper. #148 [ErGr80, p. 32] asks for good estimates of F(k), the number of solutions of 1 = 1/n1 + ⋯ + 1/nk with 1 ≤ n1 < ⋯ < nk; the page gives Konyagin's lower bound log log F(k) ≫ k/log k [Ko14] and the Elsholtz–Planitzer upper bound F(k) ≤ c0(1/5+o(1))2k [ElPl21]. #293 [ErGr80, p. 35] asks for the growth of v(k), the least integer that does not occur as a denominator in such a k-term solution. Taken literally that is 1 for every k ≥ 2, and the page reads it, after a December 2025 comment on its thread, as the least such integer above 1. Van Doorn and Tang [vDTa25b] proved log v(k) ≫ k² and noted the link to #304 through N(b−1, b). When we fetched them on 21 October, all three pages pointed to the same 14 October exposition of OpenAI's proof by Bloom and van Doorn (UnitFractions.pdf). None had a proof claim, and new comments were suspended. Our earlier work on all three is from 26 July: /report/304 (an exhaustive search proving N(b) ≤ 7 for b ≤ 1000, with equality exactly at b = 733, 739, 787, 839, 863, 898 and 907), /report/148 (F(1), …, F(7) recomputed) and /report/293 (v(1), …, v(7) and 2307 ≤ v(8) ≤ 27539; the forum has since found v(8) = 27,539 and v(9) = 13,856,993).

The claim. OpenAI's family 25, released on 6 October, is one preprint, "Short Egyptian fractions" (25 September, 33 pp). Theorem 1.1: there are absolute constants c1, c2 > 0 and b0 such that c1 log log b ≤ N(b) ≤ c2 log log b for every b ≥ b0. Corollary 1.2: ck ≤ log log F(k) ≤ Ck for every large k. Corollary 1.3: exp(exp(k/600)) ≤ v(k) ≤ 1 + k2k−1 for every large k, and more precisely log 2/257 ≤ lim inf log log v(k)/k ≤ lim sup log log v(k)/k ≤ log 2. The paper does not compute c2. The lower bound in Theorem 1.1 is Erdős's, and the upper bounds in the corollaries are elementary. The new content is the uniform upper bound for N(b), the lower bound log log F(k) ≫ k, which improves Konyagin's k/log k, and the lower bound for v(k), which improves van Doorn and Tang's exp(ck²).

The argument. Put S = log b. Section 2 reduces everything to one proposition. A greedy prefix of O(log S) terms leaves a remainder A/C with A < b and eDCS ≤ C < e2DCS (Lemma 2.3). Proposition 2.1 then supplies an integer M with eS < M ≤ eDMS and a set G of numerators u ≤ X = eDXS, missing at most X/8 of them, such that every u/(MC) with u in G is a sum of at most L log S unit fractions. Take g = ⌊X/(AM)⌋. Of the splits gAM = u + (gAM − u), at most X/4 touch a missing numerator, so one split has both parts in G. Adding the two expansions gives gA/C, and multiplying every denominator by g gives A/C. Takenouchi's 1921 replacements 2/(2p) = 1/(p+1) + 1/(p(p+1)) and 2/(2p+1) = 1/(p+1) + 1/((p+1)(2p+1)) then make the whole list distinct without lengthening it (Lemma 2.2). Sections 3–5 prove the proposition. M is the product of a power of 2 at least S100000 and of primes in [S100, 2S100]: m = ⌊S/log S⌋ fixed ones, whose 2m subset products form a deterministic list of divisors, and 1000 blocks of independently sampled ones, which form random lists. A divisor t of M gives the identity u/(MQ) = 1/((M/t)z) + (1/z)·h/(MQ), with z = ⌈Qt/u⌉ and h = uz − Qt the least residue of −Qt modulo u. One unit fraction thus replaces the numerator u by h. The descent runs through levels Xj = X·e−10⁻⁴mj, at most 3DX·10⁴·log S of them (about 6·10⁹·log S with the paper's constants). At each level, Fourier discrepancy estimates (Section 4) show that for all but an e−0.001m fraction of u, at least a ρ/2 share of the listed divisors give h below the next level. The danger is that h lands on an earlier failure. Every predecessor of a given pair (h, t) divides Qt + h, so Section 3's uniform bound Σh≤Y dX(N+h)r ≤ Y·exp(S1/4) on the r-th moment of the truncated divisor function, for every shift N ≤ eDS, controls them all at once. With Hölder's inequality it gives the recurrence δj ≤ e−0.001m + e2S1/4δj+11−1/r for the failure density, which stays below 1/8 when unrolled over the d levels. Below em the random lists reduce every numerator, and the binary expansion over the power of 2 in M finishes. For #148 (Section 6), apply the theorem to (Q−1)/Q with Q the product of the first r odd primes, append 1/Q, and split the denominator n that Q divides as 1/n = 1/(n+d) + 1/(n+n²/d) over its divisors d. This gives at least 2r−1 distinct expansions of length O(log r); the padding 1/v = 1/(v+1) + 1/(v(v+1)) on the largest denominator is injective and brings every one to length k. For #293 (Sections 7–8), reserve 1/m and use a greedy prefix that skips m. Its remainder is smaller than every reserved reciprocal, so no expansion of it can reuse a reserved denominator. The explicit slope log 2/257 comes from a second construction built on a quantitative three-primes theorem.

noncomputable def smallestCollection (a b : ℕ) : ℕ := sInf (unitFractionExpressible a b)

The paper is dated 25 September; it became public with the release on 6 October (our PDF is byte-identical to the one in openai/math's first commit). Its history skips two public intermediate results. Liam Price's 24 July proof claim on #18 gives h(n) ≪ (log log n)² for infinitely many practical n. Van Doorn and GPT-6 Astra Pro posted "Practical numbers and Egyptian fractions" on 16 September (formalised by Aristotle), with N(b) ≤ 2c0(log log b)² and v(k) ≥ exp(exp(√(k/(2c0)))), c0 = 14/log 2. Both are strictly weaker than the paper's theorems, so this is a gap in its exposition, not a priority question. Bloom and van Doorn cite the second. We found no earlier public claim of any of the three double-logarithmic results. Bloom and van Doorn's exposition flags no mathematical error. It says that the technical arguments of §§3–5, "occupying 15 pages, out of 33 total", are "entirely redundant", because the paper's own §7–8 method gives Theorem 1.1 more simply, and "Strangely, nowhere in the paper is this oddity mentioned". It notes that the lower bound for F(k) follows at once from the one for v(k), since v(k) ≤ kF(k) + 2. It replaces the paper's grouping in §8.2 with base-B digits and the five-product Fourier argument with the Hart–Iosevich sum-product theorem, and gets the length (61/log 2 + o(1))·log log b.

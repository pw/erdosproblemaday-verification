- E1 (p. 75 l. 43; p. 76 ll. 6–12). "Initially L_wt = 1; after the first active step choose it larger than s." versus "Multiplying all absolute weights by a sufficiently large fixed integer makes the enlarged gap absorb that other cost" and "the required weight multiples ... are fixed by a recursion of depth s". Two distinct requirements (L > s for triangular order against determining slots; L ≥ K for the gap argument, K depending on the restored-formula degree, which itself depends on earlier inflations) are stated in separate sentences, and the first sentence reads as a single multiplier while the argument needs one per active step. Repairable as written elsewhere in the paragraph.
- E2 (p. 183 l. 49; p. 179 l. 37 vs. p. 64 l. 48, p. 64 l. 18, p. 67 l. 41, p. 65 l. 10). Prop I.6 is stated for "π : G^0 → Y of degree r ≤ s" and Prop I.1 for "degree 1 ≤ d ≤ s", but §8 invokes "the marked-symbol argument of Proposition I.6", "the partner-selection part of Proposition I.6" and "the reconstruction argument of Proposition I.6" for a G^0 whose degree r (max weight deficit) exceeds s once absolute weights are inflated (Def 7.2(iii), p. 45), with "only r grades" in the matching. Covered by the global convention "A degree bound may be replaced by any fixed larger one" (p. 8) and §9.6's "take all sampler and algebra degree bounds large enough", but the appendix statements should carry a general fixed degree bound.
- E3 (p. 61 l. 43). "Suppose their signed score ... is at least δ > 0" does not say "on each such path"; the proof (p. 62 "Productive path fractions, unlike the score in (8.2), may decrease") uses it per path. Also the sign convention (F^− carries the flag and is the positive payoff) is the cutoff convention of §7.1 and could be stated at (8.2).
- E4 (p. 190 l. 32 vs. l. 48; p. 192 l. 2). Copies are described as "a direct sum of the original coordinates and copies from higher tags" (I.11), as "tagged copies of the original coordinate space" ℝ^D (I.12), and bounded by "a fixed polynomial majorant of D(1+jD)^j". - E5 (p. 188 l. 35). "h ≤ min(1/32, ε/L)" gives ‖Φ̃−Φ‖_∞ ≤ ε only if L is the max-norm Lipschitz constant (as stated in Lemma I.10); with a Euclidean constant one needs an extra √D. Absorbed in early bounds.
- E6 (style, pervasive). §8 and App. I are written at sketch density ("as in Proposition I.6", "Preserving the two mesh conditions", pp. 67–68). I traced each such reference to the corresponding step and found it applies, but a journal referee would require the mesh-preservation paragraph and the forecasting-locus identity (p. 65, (8.7)) to be expanded.

## D1 hand-off: Prop I.1 without Property 1
(Appended by the "inputs" seat (Appendices A–D) at the coordinator's request. The same text, with details, is in referee_inputs.md under "Hand-off".)

**Property 1 of Prop. F.2** (p. 137) has four parts:
- (1a) "Each ψ(T) lies in U";
- (1b) "the functions y_h∘ψ are fully slow polynomial lifts while β_h∘ψ have integer ordinary coefficients, all of degrees at most h";
- (1c) "Noninjective maps have negligible probability";
- (1d) "If the centers are mixed independently with Haar measure, the one-site law, with t uniform, approximates the uniform law on U at the prescribed forward accuracy against every unit-bounded input."

**What the §3 sampler supplies** (Lemma 3.2, p. 19):
- item 1 gives (1b) verbatim ("C(ψ(t)) − c = y(ψ(t)) + β(ψ(t)) is a polynomial identity. The small lift is fully slow at width v; β∘ψ has integral monomial coefficients");
- item 3 gives (1c) and support on the buffered chart;
- item 4 gives only a warm-strip version of (1d).
- So the missing ingredient (§3.4) is the fine-accuracy (1d).

**What the proof of Prop. I.1 (pp. 182–183) uses**, read to the end:
- (1b): "Since the full substituted polynomial has ordinary degree at most d, the pure degree-d part in the retained variables is independent of the values fixed on short axes. … Cancel the remaining lower-degree terms by an ordinary polynomial phase of degree at most d − 1."
- Paths inside {χ = 1}: "Choose χ to equal one on all path supports and to vanish outside the buffered charts. The local correlation is then at least 9/10."
- Property 2 at fixed centres: "Property 2 of the sampler, applied with degree d − 1, gives an early positive ambient correlation with a site twist times a degree-(d − 1) niltest."
- After that, everything happens on the ambient U: Fourier expansion on ambient covers, Lemma B.1 on the product group, the rank hypothesis ("an integer row forbidden by high rank"), and Lemmas I.3, I.4, A.5.
- Mixed centres, the one-site law, and noninjectivity never appear. Remark I.5 confirms this: "The preceding proof uses Property 2 of Proposition F.2 and Lemma B.1 with their stated early bounds."

**Conclusion.** Property 1(d), the part the §3 sampler lacks, is never used in the proof of Prop. I.1. The parts that are used are supplied by Lemma 3.2 items 1 and 3. This is **EXPOSITION**: the hypothesis "Properties 1 and 2" in Prop. I.1 should become "(1b), chart-supported paths, Property 2, rank ≥ R". It is not a GAP at Lemma 4.7.
- Caveat (outside both of our scopes): this relies on the cold clause of Theorem 3.4 (the §3 substitute for Property 2) being proved without fine (1d).
- The verdict line above is unchanged.

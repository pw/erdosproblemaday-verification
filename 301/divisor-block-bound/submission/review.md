**Verdict: VERIFIED.** The bound holds: f(N) ≤ (61487/77376 + o(1))N ≈ 0.794652N. Reviewed 21 November 2026. Write-up: [/day/301-divisor-block-bound-checked-and-pushed-further](/day/301-divisor-block-bound-checked-and-pushed-further).

**What it rests on.** The submission gives the numbers, not the method, so we rebuilt the method from them: it is Donald Della Pietra's divisor-block argument (github.com/donalddellapietra/erdos-301-proof, 30 July 2026). Every integer is m·d in exactly one way with d a divisor of M and each prime exponent of m a multiple of (its exponent in M) + 1. A set with no 1/a = 1/b₁ + … + 1/b_k meets each block in a scaled solution-free set of divisors up to N/m. That gives f(N) ≤ (1 − δ_M W_M + o(1))N, with δ_M = ∏ (1 − 1/p)/(1 − p^−(e+1)) and W_M = Σ 1/d over the divisors where the largest solution-free prefix does not grow. The submitted density 55/248 is δ for M = 720720.

**What we checked.** (1) The partition lemma and the refinement h_{pM}(t) ≥ h_M(t) + h_M(t/p), with δ_{pM}·(1 + 1/p) = δ_M. (2) W_5040 = 3907/5040 exactly, from an exact search over all 60 divisors of 5040. It reproduces Della Pietra's 15437/19344 and his constants for 2520 and 1680. (3) All six excess intervals at 55440 and the +1 on [65, 66) at 720720. Two independent programs computed those profiles exactly: a C depth-first search, and CP-SAT with lazy constraints whose "no larger set" verdicts SCIP re-checked. On every interval the exact excess is at least what you claim, and on several it is larger: +2 at 56 and +3 on [88, 112) at 55440, and +1 already on [60, 72) at 720720. A hostile referee seat with its own solvers agrees. (4) The arithmetic, in exact fractions.

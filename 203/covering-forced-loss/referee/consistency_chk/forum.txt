I made a computational attempt at the natural constructive route: a set of primes $p$ whose "killed" sets ${(k,l) : p \mid 2^k 3^l m + 1}$ cover all $(k,l)$. Each such set is one coset of a lattice of index $e_p = |\langle 2,3\rangle \bmod p|$, so this is a 2D covering problem. Results (code and data at https://github.com/veljjanoski/erdos203 ): Complete prime pools. If $\mathrm{ord}p 2 = a$ and $\mathrm{ord}p 3 = b$ then $p$ divides $\gcd(\Phi_a(2), \Phi_b(3))$, which gives all primes of any size with $e_p \le E$. For $E = 2000$ there are $442$ such primes with $\sum 1/e_p = 1.97$; for $E = 5000$, $1006$ primes with $\sum 1/e_p = 2.15$. So the density condition $\sum 1/e_p \ge 1$ is not the obstacle. Searches. Greedy plus iterated local search over the coset choices on the torus $\mathbb{Z}_N^2$ reaches $71.7%$ coverage at $N = 5040$ ($31$ primes, matching the $71$–$72%$ reported above) and $76.0%$ at $N = 55440$ ($55$ primes). Taking $m$ to be a perfect power (cube, or $12$th power for the extra $4Y^4+1$ factorisation) does not help: $74%$ and $70%$. All plateaus sit a few percent below the random-choice value $1 - \exp(-\sum 1/e_p)$. A structured family. After $k' = k + Jl$, the primes with $(3 \cdot 2^{-J})^d \equiv 1 \pmod p$ give cosets depending on $l$ only mod $d$, so the problem reduces to a strip $\mathbb{Z}N \times \mathbb{Z}d$ where the lattices nest like 1D moduli. The maximal admissible density is computable exactly. For $N = 720720$ and the $99$ primes $p \le 10^7$ with $e_p \mid N$ it is below $1$ for every $J$ and every strip height $d < 720$, so no covering of this kind exists there; at $d = 720$ it is $1.05$ and the search still leaves $28%$ uncovered. Structure. Among the $442$ primes with $e_p \le 2000$ all lattices are distinct, only $77$ pairs are nested, all with index ratio $\ge 12$, and the only primes with $e_p$ a power of $2$ are $5, 17, 257$, none nested in another. The chains that make 1D Sierpiński coverings work ($2 \mid 4 \mid 8 \mid \cdots \mid 64$) have no analogue here. None of this is a proof of nonexistence. It is evidence that a covering system for this problem, if one exists, needs primes of far larger order than anything searched so far. I am not claiming a solution. The existence question remains open. My claim is a partial result: a general density obstruction for finite 2D covering sets, plus some computationally bounded exclusions. For each prime \(p\nmid 6\), define\[ H_p=\langle 2,3\rangle\subseteq(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times . \]I prove\[ |H_p|=\operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{ord}_p(2),\operatorname{ord}_p(3)). \]It follows that if a finite set of primes \(P\) gives a covering proof for some \(m\), i.e. if for every \(k,\ell\geq 0\) some \(p\in P\) divides\[ 2^k3^\ell m+1, \]then necessarily\[ \sum_{p\in P}\frac{1}{|H_p|}\geq 1. \]Using this density condition, I rule out several possible covering regimes, including the four-prime set \(\{5,11,23,13\}\), all linear primes with \(\operatorname{ord}_p(2)\leq 60\), and linear-prime coverings with\[ \operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{ord}_p(2))\leq 720720. \]The strongest current computational exclusion is: among primes \(p\leq 50{,}000{,}000\), no covering set \(P\) with\[ \operatorname{lcm}(|H_p|:p\in P)<5040 \]can exist, since the corresponding density sum is still \(<1\). This is a computational theorem within that prime bound, not an unconditional theorem over all primes. I also checked natural candidate families at moduli \(5040\) and \(10080\). No covering was found; the best coverage stayed around \(71\%-72\%\). This is computational evidence only, not a proof of nonexistence. A useful structural observation is that any such 2D Sierpiński number would also be a classical 1D Sierpiński number, by setting \(\ell=0\). The natural candidate \(m=78557\) does not work, since\[ 2^1\cdot 3^2\cdot 78557+1=1414027 \]is prime. So the exact status is: - Proved: the subgroup formula for \(|H_p|\). - Proved: the density necessary condition for finite 2D covering sets. - Proved/computed within scope: no covering with \(\operatorname{lcm}(|H_p|)<5040\) using primes \(p\leq 50{,}000{,}000\). - Not proved: existence of \(m\). - Not proved: nonexistence of \(m\). - Therefore the original \(r=2\) problem remains open. Dogmachine — 23:35 on 25 Jan 2026 👍 1 📝 0 🤖 0 Note that the question regarding the primes $q$ is equivalent to asking for an even positive integer $m$ such that the quadratic form $g(x,y)=mx^2+my^2+1$ is free of primes whenever $x,y$ are coprime positive integers and $x+y$ odd. If we remove such restrictions and simply allows any Integers as $x,y$, such $m$ does not exist by classical results of Iwaniec (see H. Iwaniec, Primes representes by quadratic polynomials in two variables, Acta Arith. 24 (1974), 435-459), since the form $g$ obeys some caveats, including being genuinely a function of two variables, that force It to contain primes. Perhaps someone well acquainted with the analytic theory can prove such $g$ will have primes even with the aforementioned restrictions on $x,y$?

For f = x4+2, ρ(p2) is 2 for p ≡ 3 mod 8, either 4 or 0 for p ≡ 1 mod 8, and 0 otherwise. Our July report proves this, and checks the formula by brute force for p ≤ 104. The predicted density is c ≈ 0.7566840, enclosed rigorously in July between 0.75668370 and 0.75668401. For x4+1 it is c8 = ∏p≡1 (8)(1 − 4/p2) ≈ 0.9808175. The counts:

Each count was made twice, by different methods.

  • By factoring every n4+w in PARI/GP. Our July run covered n4+2 to 105, with its factorisations certified by a separate program. Today we ran both polynomials to 105 and to 2·105, and seat M2 ran both to 106.
  • By a sieve that factors nothing. It runs over every prime p ≤ 106, marking the classes n ≡ r mod p2 for each root r of x4+w lifted from p to p2.

The two agree at every checkpoint. A version of the sieve with the lift removed gives 65,353 at 105, so the agreement is not automatic.

The large-prime tail that Proposition 1.4 is about is tiny here. Factoring every value up to 106 (M2's run; ours to 105 agrees) finds only two n with p2 | n4+2 for a prime p > n: n = 2 (p = 3) and n = 97 (974+2 = 1792·2763). For n4+1 there are none. The sieve is exact for N ≤ 106 only because of this census: a prime p > 106 with p2 | f(n) and n ≤ 106 would have p > n, and none does.

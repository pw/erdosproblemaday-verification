The problem (erdosproblems.com/604, $500; Erdős 1957 Problem 16 and eleven later papers). Given n distinct points A ⊂ ℝ², must there be a point x ∈ A determining ≫ n1−o(1) distinct distances to the others — or even ≫ n/√log n? The pinned form of the distinct-distances problem (#89, which Guth–Katz nearly closed in 2015 for the union of all distances). The √n × √n grid shows n/√log n would be best possible; Harborth showed the pinned answer is not simply the global one; the page's best known bound is Katz–Tardos 2004, ≫ nc−o(1) with c = (48 − 14e)/(55 − 16e) = 0.864137…, the end of the Solymosi–Tóth n6/7 → Tardos → Katz–Tardos line (the last two by entropy inequalities). One comment (Hunter: one such point gives ≫ n of them greedily); zero proof claims; last edited 23 March 2026. The teorth database says open (last updated 31 Aug 2025); Formal Conjectures' erdos_604 states exactly the n1−ε form — for every ε > 0 and all large n, the least over n-sets of the largest pin count is at least n1−ε — and labels it research open, with Katz–Tardos as a solved variant.

The claim. OpenAI's 23 September 2026 preprint "The weak pinned planar distance theorem" (27 pages, family 167 of the 6 October release, the sibling of the unit-distance power-saving paper we refereed this morning; on Bloom's 8 October list of 25 as "Pinned distances … This proof has been formalised"). Theorem 1.1: for n ≥ 2 and s > 0 let Fn(s) be the largest possible fraction of ordered pairs (x, y) of distinct points of an n-point planar set for which the distance ‖y − x‖ occurs at least ns times among the distances from x; then Fn(s) → 0 for every fixed s, uniformly over all configurations, with no rate. Corollary 1.2 (half a page): for every fixed ε > 0 the fraction of points seeing fewer than n1−ε distinct distances tends to zero — so every sufficiently large planar set has a point with at least n1−ε distinct distances, and in fact all but o(n) of its points do. That is the page's first question answered yes, every exponent below 1 at once, replacing 0.864; it says nothing about n/√log n, and the centre of a circle shows the exceptional set is needed. The name is from Dewar–Frankl–Mansfield–Nixon–Passant–Warren (May 2025, Conjecture 4.7, "weak pinned distance conjecture" — a single point with Cε|P|1−ε distances), who proved that the weak unit-distance conjecture would imply it; that route died in May 2026 when OpenAI's n1+c unit-distance construction refuted the hypothesis, and this paper claims a direct proof.

The architecture is a contrapositive with two compactness regimes, and the arithmetic is the surprise. If the theorem fails, a density of pairs sits in large distance fibers; §2 extracts, from configurations attaining the supremum, a directed graph of positive density θ whose every fiber has ≥ ns−o(1) edges, after first moving the points to real algebraic coordinates (transfer for real closed fields keeps every distance equality and disequality) — so all coordinates live in a number field K ∋ i. §3 writes Z1 = u + ib, Z2 = u − ib, factors ‖y − x‖² = (Z1(y) − Z1(x))(Z2(y) − Z2(x)), so that at every place the two logarithmic depths on a fiber sum to a constant while the product formula of K makes their weighted sum over all places vanish on every pair; after building nested partitions at each place (the ultrametric balls at finite places; randomly shifted and rotated square grids at the complex ones, whose depth error has a pair-independent law with exponential tails) and replacing any cell holding more than half the points by its complement, the overlap of two points summed over all levels and places is additive: L*(x, y) = b* + S*(x) + S*(y). Any signed law on distinct pairs with zero mass and zero marginal sum then integrates to zero against the overlap kernel — and the uniform law on a fiber, minus twice a law with one fiber-uniform and one ambient-uniform marginal, plus the uniform law on all pairs, is such a law. §4 shows cells of size ≤ n1−c carry a negligible share of the overlap scale W (a cell containing a source but a small part of its fiber is controlled through the other coordinate, where the rest of the fiber sits in the complementary cell); §5 turns the vanishing integral into a variance bound: the mass a fiber puts on a cell is, on average, the cell's uniform mass, up to o(W + 1). If W → ∞ (§6), the cells of intermediate mass form weighted trees of total length ≍ W; the fiber equation converts the distance from a source to its target in one coordinate's tree into a root-to-target distance in the other, transport on the trees replaces two sources of one target by independent uniform points, and an elementary lower bound on tree-distance fluctuations contradicts o(W). If W stays bounded (§7), one complex embedding is normalized so that both coordinate laws have nonatomic weak limits β1, β2; the variance bound makes fiber laws and incoming-neighbour laws converge to the same limits, so a limiting edge carries a Möbius map Φ(z) = b + (a′ − a)(b′ − b)/(z − a) with β2 = Φ*β1 and the source coordinate a independent of the target pair — which Lemma 7.1 shows cannot happen (let a approach a′ inside the support: the pushed-forward logarithmic distance collapses while β2's has spread). The Euclidean structure enters through the factorization (3.1) into two coordinates that are injective on real points (so distinct points have nonzero product); the characteristic-zero input is §7's archimedean place — which is the right shape: over 𝔽q every point of 𝔽q² sees only √n distances.

What the seats found. Eight tool-equipped referee seats, each briefed to refute — six on disjoint stretches of the paper (§1–2 · §3 · §4 · §5 and §6.1 · §6.2–6.4 · §7), one on the whole paper as a dependency graph with the Lean statement, one on sources and the world — read all 27 pages from the rendered PDF, lemma by lemma, and every one returned HOLDS AS READ: no FATAL, no GAP, forty-odd cosmetic notes. Each seat rebuilt something and made it fail on purpose. The §2 seat computed F3 and F4 exactly (the rhombus of two equilateral triangles is the extremal 4-set), ran the deletion-process inequality on 200,000 exact random instances, and confirmed that the small-subset regime of the subset estimate is legitimate because the count uses fibers restricted to the subset. The §3 seat fetched Milne's notes and matched the product-formula normalization weight for weight (the factor 2 in the complex weight 2/[K:ℚ] is Milne's squared modulus |σa|²; the archimedean weights sum to one because i ∈ K forbids real places), tested the pair-independence of the grid error by Kolmogorov–Smirnov across pairs whose separations differ by five orders of magnitude (p = 0.40–0.82; removing the random scale, rotation or an independent axis shift drops p below 10−93), computed κ = −1.4572 by quadrature and Monte Carlo, and verified the additive identity end to end on seven points of ℚ(i) (constant across all 21 pairs; dropping the finite places or the complex factor 2 breaks it). The §4 seat replayed Lemma 4.1's three cases in exact rational arithmetic on 397 random double hierarchies with every identity at zero discrepancy; the §5 seat verified the signed identity and the exact cell-probability formula (5.4) term for term — they match the Lean's signedCell_formula — on 400 random instances (the integrated form on 150 hierarchies) with six controls that fail; the §6 seat checked Lemma 6.1 exactly over all triples on 2,478 random trees (smallest margin 288×; the paper's constant is of order δ³/512 where the truth is about δ², and the Lean proves (δ/2)³L by a short minimal-cell argument instead) and verified the fiber equation (6.10) at a p-adic place and at a complex embedding where the two coordinates are not conjugates; the §7 seat confirmed that the bars in (7.1) are |log|·|| (our text extraction had dropped them; with the signed reading the small-ball bound fails outright on explicit 1,200-point sets, by factors of 300 and 1,100) and illustrated Lemma 7.1 numerically (the left side of (7.8) falls from 1.32 to 4.5·10−4 as a → a′ against a fixed right side of 1.48; with an atom at a′ it does not). The cosmetic yield, for whoever edits the paper: the axis shifts must be independent (the Lean's axisLaw.prod axisLaw says so; the paper does not); the condition c < s*/3 is never used; H ≥ 1 can be H ≥ 0; the bound ΣσvTv ≤ C comes from (3.7), not Lemma 5.1; "−log|Z − b′| is nonconstant" needs b′ in the support, which the previous sentence fixes; the singleton-leaf edges of the cell tree have zero length only because w(1/n) = 0 once n ≥ 2/δ, which is said on p. 19 but not where the tree is built. Four findings are worth more than cosmetics. First, the range s > 0.1359 of Theorem 1.1 already follows in two lines from Katz–Tardos, and the paper does not say so: Katz–Tardos's Corollary 8 bounds the isosceles triangles by O(n(117−34e)/(55−16e)+ε) = O(n2.1359+ε), and a two-line count (every rich pair sits in a fiber of size ≥ ns, a fiber of size f carries f(f−1)/2 apex-isosceles pairs) gives Fn(s) → 0 for every s > 0.1359 — checked exactly on 121 (configuration, threshold) cases from 11 small configurations, with a failing control — so the theorem's new content is exactly the range 0 < s ≤ 0.1359, and Corollary 1.2 needs it only for ε ≤ 0.272. Second, "all but o(n) pins" adds nothing to "one pin": Hunter's greedy comment on the problem page already turns one good pin into ≫ n, and with ε → 2ε into all but o(n). Third, the Lean does not follow the prose: its §2 transfers to the algebraic numbers by the Nullstellensatz rather than to the real algebraic numbers by real-closed-field transfer, replaces the greedy deletion by maximising a penalised score, and its arithmetic contradiction consumes only two injective coordinate maps, nonzero fiber products constant on each fiber, and the graph properties; its bounded-scale obstruction uses a cross-ratio energy rather than the paper's functional Jw; its tree lemma has a different constant. So the kernel certifies the theorem; the seats' reading supports the prose, which the Lean follows in architecture (§§3–5 and the route of §7) but replaces in §2, in the §6 weights and tree lemma, and in the §7 obstruction. Fourth, and the whole seat's own finding: the argument never uses that Z2 is the conjugate of Z1 — the paper says so on p. 3 and p. 9 — so what it proves verbatim is a Theorem G: for any finite set with two injective coordinate maps into the algebraic numbers and the "distance" (Z1(y) − Z1(x))(Z2(y) − Z2(x)), all but o(n) points see n1−ε distinct values — which covers the real Minkowski plane (no two points on a common light-like line) and the bilinear distance on ℂ² (no two on a common isotropic line), neither stated in the paper; the Lean's core contradiction arithmetic_sequence_impossible is stated at exactly that generality (an abstract finite type, injective maps into a NumberField, a purely combinatorial PreparedGraph), so a counterexample to Theorem G, once put through §2's extraction (field-agnostic on paper, formalized only for the plane), would contradict a kernel-checked theorem. The seat searched Roche-Newton–Rudnev, Sheffer–Zahl, Dewar et al. and Lee–Pohoata–Zhu for one and found none. The sanity test the brief demanded — over 𝔽q every point of 𝔽q² sees √n distances, so a correct proof must use something false there — comes out the right way: for q ≡ 1 (mod 4) the proof breaks at §3.1 (Z1 is not injective and products vanish on distinct pairs); for q ≡ 3 (mod 4) every hypothesis of §§3–5 holds in 𝔽q²(T) with W = 0, and the proof breaks exactly at §7.1, which needs an archimedean place and one compact Riemann sphere for every configuration. And the factorization itself is load-bearing: the √n × √n grid in the ℓ∞ metric has √n distances at every pin, so the Lean's choice of EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 2) over Fin 2 → ℝ (which would make dist the sup metric) is the difference between a theorem and a falsehood. One wording note for the scoreboard: exists_pin_eventually counts nonzero distances, the page's #{d(x, y) : y ∈ A} includes d(x, x) = 0 — a difference of one, as Formal Conjectures' docstring also notes.

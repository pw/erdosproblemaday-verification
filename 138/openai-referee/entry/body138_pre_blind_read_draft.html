The problem (erdosproblems.com/138, $500; Erdős 1957, 1961, 1973–1981, 1997 and the Erdős–Graham books). Let W(k) be the least N such that every two-colouring of {1, …, N} contains a monochromatic k-term arithmetic progression. "Improve the bounds for W(k) — for example, prove that W(k)1/k → ∞." Erdős offered the $500 for a proof or disproof of that limit; in the same survey he asked the weaker W(k)/2k → ∞. The page's record on 15 October: Berlekamp's W(p+1) ≥ p·2p for primes p; Kozik–Shabanov's W(k) ≫ 2k as "the best general lower bound"; Gowers's tower upper bound; the DeepMind prover's W(k+1) ≥ W(k) + k; and, for r ≥ 3 colours, Fox–Hunter's W3(k)1/k ≥ Clog* k (June 2026), which the page calls the resolution of "the analogous question for r ≥ 3". The two-colour case — the case Erdős priced — is open on the page. Since its last edit (2 June) one comment, 24 August, links Campos–Fox–Schildkraut's W(k) ≥ (1 − o(1))k·2k−1 (arXiv 2608.20824, five pages, colouring found by ChatGPT 5.6 Sol Pro), which settles W(k)/2k → ∞ and not the k-th root. The proof-claims tab is empty.

The claim. OpenAI's 23 September 2026 preprint "Quantitative Superexponential Bounds for van der Waerden Numbers" (25 pages, family 160 of the 6 October release; on Bloom's 8 October list of 25 and among the twelve he marks as FrontierMath Erdős-list problems). Theorem 1.1: there is an absolute integer K0 such that for every integer k ≥ K0 and every integer r ≥ 2, Wr(k) > kck⌊log2 r⌋ with c = 10−5; hence Wr(k)1/k → ∞ for each fixed r ≥ 2, "including the two-color case". The threshold is one number for all r. Two further theorems ride along: Wr(k) > exp((log r)2/(64 log 2)) for every r ≥ 256 and k ≥ 3 (Theorem 7.2, a Behrend digits-and-sphere colouring, superpolynomial growth in the number of colours), and an elementary recursive finiteness bound (Appendix A) with the boundary values W1(k) = k, Wr(1) = 1, Wr(2) = r + 1.

The architecture is a two-colouring of one cyclic group, lifted to every number of colours by a digit product; everything new lives in §2–§6. Fix c = 10−5, D = ⌈k1/10⌉, M = ⌈k1/2⌉, h0 = D2, p = k−1/20, H = k−2, η = 1/(1000D). Take a prime P ∈ (k, 2k2], the least power q of P with q ≥ kck/D, and G = ℤ/qDℤ, so |G| ≥ kck. Each n ∈ G gets two systems of circle coordinates, xi(n) = n/qi mod 1 and yi(n) = λxi(n), where the integer dilation λ = ∏ℓ ≤ h0 ℓ⌊log(2M)/log ℓ⌋ contains the full ℓ-primary part of every period h ≤ 2M at every prime ℓ ≤ D2 — so after dilation a rational period is either exactly 1 or larger than D2 (Lemma 2.1), the separation the whole argument turns on. The x-circles are cut into uniform intervals of width H/λ, the y-circles into a geometric mesh that gets finer toward the cut ±½ used to pick centred representatives ỹ ∈ [−½, ½)D (Lemma 2.3: width ≍ H·(distance to the cut + α), α = q−(D+1)). A point's label is its 2D-tuple of intervals. §3 counts: the number of label words along a k-term progression is exp(O(k3/10 log k)) (one hyperplane arrangement in the two scalar parameters start and step, per coordinate — Lemma 3.1 counts equality signs too, for the half-open intervals), and the number of "eligible local patterns" of period h through one specified label is at most (CDh)6D with C absolute and independent of q, λ, and the width of the anchor interval (Lemma 3.4 — the stationary drift is bounded relative to the interval width, which is what makes the count survive arbitrarily fine mesh). §4 proves a finite asymmetric local lemma and uses it to pick one outer colouring c* of all labels such that every progression with ≥ k/10 light positions (label multiplicity ≤ k/M) sees each bit on a quarter of them, and every eligible pattern is balanced (weights e−l/32, incident weight per label ≤ 1/500, criterion 1/8 − 1/32 − 2/500 > 0 at l ≥ 100). §5 is the dichotomy: every progression either carries ≥ k/100 of each outer bit, or its centred y-representatives are globally affine in j. A heavy label's closest return fixes a rational period h ≤ 2M and a drift (Lemmas 5.2–5.3; the gcd argument needs the representative box to be a product of half-open intervals); if h ≤ D2 the dilation makes the y-motion a pure real step w ∈ q−iℤ with total motion < ½, and a cut crossing would shrink every mesh interval in that coordinate below |wi| and make all labels distinct — contradicting the heavy label, so the lift is affine; if D2 < h ≤ 2M, each full period block containing a heavy index is an eligible pattern (regular fraction ≥ 1 − D(4η + 1/h0) > ½), and the balanced patterns cover four fifths of the progression. §6 perturbs: a key is a y-box together with the integer band ⌊‖qỹ‖22⌋; any two distinct terms of a progression are ≥ 1 apart after scaling by q (the coordinate where λ(nj − nj′) first fails to vanish mod q), so a norm band meets the affine line through a box's points in two segments of length ≤ 1 and a key occurs at most four times along any progression (Lemma 6.1, Behrend's annulus in a bounded-width form). Flip an independent Bernoulli(p) bit per key. A progression rich in both outer colours needs ≥ k/400 distinct keys flipped: probability ≤ pk/400, and the union over |G|2 progressions has exponent (2c − 1/8000)k log k = −(21/200000)k log k → −∞. An affine progression's signature (outer bits and literal keys) is determined by its label word and a quadratic in j compared with ≤ k(Dq2 + 2) integers — exp(O(k9/10 log k)) signatures, each needing ≥ k/4 distinct keys to all land one way, probability ≤ (1 − p)k/4 = exp(−k19/20/4); identical signatures are the same event. Both unions are o(1), so some choice of bits colours G with no monochromatic nonzero-step progression (Theorem 6.3). §7: base-qD digits with that cyclic colouring in every digit give W2m(k) > qDm — the lowest digit at which the step is nonzero is a cyclic progression with nonzero step — hence Wr(k) > qD⌊log2 r⌋ ≥ kck⌊log2 r⌋. The threshold on k is fixed in §2–§6 before r appears, which is the uniformity. The exponent 10−5 is where the two unions are made to clear: 2c must sit below γ/80 with γ = 1/100, and nothing in the paper claims it is close to the truth.

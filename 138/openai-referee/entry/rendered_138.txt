Bloom calls these claims, not endorsements. The problem ( erdosproblems.com/138 , $500; Erdős 1957, 1961, 1973–1981, 1997 and Erdős–Graham 1979 and 1980). Let W ( k ) be the least N such that every two-colouring of {1, …, N } contains a monochromatic k -term arithmetic progression. "Improve the bounds for W ( k ) — for example, prove that W ( k ) 1/ k → ∞." Erdős offered the $500 for a proof or disproof of that limit; in the same survey he asked the weaker W ( k )/2 k → ∞. The page's record on 15 October: Berlekamp's W ( p +1) ≥ p ·2 p for primes p ; Kozik–Shabanov's W ( k ) ≫ 2 k as "the best general lower bound"; Gowers's tower upper bound; the DeepMind prover's W ( k +1) ≥ W ( k ) + k ; and, for r ≥ 3 colours, Fox–Hunter's W 3 ( k ) 1/ k ≥ C log* k (June 2026), which the page calls the resolution of "the analogous question for r ≥ 3". The two-colour case — the case Erdős priced — is unresolved in the page's text (the site has displayed no statuses since 6 October). Since its last edit (2 June) one comment, 24 August, links Campos–Fox–Schildkraut's W ( k ) ≥ (1 − o (1)) k ·2 k −1 (arXiv 2608.20824, five pages, colouring found by ChatGPT 5.6 Sol Pro), which settles W ( k )/2 k → ∞ and not the k -th root. OpenAI's 23 September 2026 preprint "Quantitative Superexponential Bounds for van der Waerden Numbers" (25 pages, family 160 of the 6 October release; on Bloom's 8 October list of 25 and among the twelve he marks as FrontierMath Erdős-list problems). Theorem 1.1: there is an absolute integer K 0 such that for every integer k ≥ K 0 and every integer r ≥ 2, W r ( k ) &gt; k ck ⌊log 2 r ⌋ with c = 10 −5 ; hence W r ( k ) 1/ k → ∞ for each fixed r ≥ 2 — in the abstract's words, "including the two-color case". The threshold is one number for all r . Two further theorems ride along: W r ( k ) &gt; exp((log r ) 2 /(64 log 2)) for every r ≥ 256 and k ≥ 3 (Theorem 7.2, a Behrend digits-and-sphere colouring, superpolynomial growth in the number of colours), and an elementary recursive finiteness bound (Appendix A) with the boundary values W 1 ( k ) = k , W r (1) = 1, W r (2) = r + 1. The architecture is a two-colouring of one cyclic group, lifted to every number of colours by a digit product; everything new lives in §2–§6. Fix c = 10 −5 , D = ⌈ k 1/10 ⌉, M = ⌈ k 1/2 ⌉, h 0 = D 2 , p = k −1/20 , H = k −2 , η = 1/(1000 D ). Take a prime P ∈ ( k , 2 k 2 ], the least power q of P with q ≥ k ck / D , and G = ℤ/ q D ℤ, so | G | ≥ k ck . Each n ∈ G gets two systems of circle coordinates, x i ( n ) = n / q i mod 1 and y i ( n ) = λ x i ( n ), where the integer dilation λ = ∏ ℓ ≤ h 0 ℓ ⌊log(2 M )/log ℓ⌋ contains the full ℓ-primary part of every period h ≤ 2 M at every prime ℓ ≤ D 2 — so after dilation a rational period is either exactly 1 or larger than D 2 (Lemma 2.1), the separation the whole argument turns on. The x -circles are cut into uniform intervals of width H /λ, the y -circles into a geometric mesh that gets finer toward the cut ±½ used to pick centred representatives ỹ ∈ [−½, ½) D (Lemma 2.3: width ≍ H ·(distance to the cut + α), α = q −( D +1) ). A point's label is its 2 D -tuple of intervals. §3 counts: the number of distinct label words realized by k -term progressions is exp( O ( k 3/10 log k )) (one hyperplane arrangement in the two scalar parameters start and step, per coordinate — Lemma 3.1 counts equality signs too, for the half-open intervals), and the number of "eligible local patterns" of period h through one specified label is at most ( CDh ) 6 D with C absolute and independent of q , λ, and the width of the anchor interval (Lemma 3.4 — the stationary drift is bounded relative to the interval width , which is what makes the count survive arbitrarily fine mesh). §4 proves a finite asymmetric local lemma and uses it to pick one outer colouring c * of all labels such that every progression with ≥ k /10 light positions (label multiplicity ≤ k / M ) sees each bit on a quarter of them, and every eligible pattern is balanced (weights e − l /32 , incident weight per label ≤ 1/500, criterion (1/8 − 1/32 − 2/500)· l ≥ log 2 for l ≥ 100). §5 is the dichotomy: every progression either carries ≥ k /100 of each outer bit, or its centred y -representatives are globally affine in j . A heavy label's closest return fixes a rational period h ≤ 2 M and a drift (Lemmas 5.2–5.3; the gcd argument needs the representative box to be a product of half-open intervals); if h ≤ D 2 the dilation makes the y -motion a pure real step w ∈ q − i ℤ with total motion &lt; ½, and a cut crossing would shrink every mesh interval in that coordinate below | w i | and make all labels distinct — contradicting the heavy label, so the lift is affine; if D 2 &lt; h ≤ 2 M , each full period block containing a heavy index is an eligible pattern (regular fraction ≥ 1 − D (4η + 1/ h 0 ) &gt; ½), and the balanced patterns cover four fifths of the progression. §6 perturbs: a key is a y -box together with the integer band ⌊‖ q ỹ‖ 2 2 ⌋; any two distinct terms of a progression are ≥ 1 apart after scaling by q (the coordinate where λ( n j − n j ′ ) first fails to vanish mod q ), so a norm band meets the affine line through a box's points in two segments of length ≤ 1 and a key occurs at most four times along any progression (Lemma 6.1, Behrend's annulus in a bounded-width form). A progression rich in both outer colours needs ≥ k /400 distinct keys flipped: probability ≤ p k /400 , and the union over | G | 2 progressions has exponent (2 c − 1/8000) k log k = −(21/200000) k log k → −∞. An affine progression's signature (outer bits and literal keys) is determined by its label word and a quadratic in j compared with ≤ k ( Dq 2 + 2) integers — exp( O ( k 9/10 log k )) signatures, each needing ≥ k /4 distinct keys to all land one way, probability ≤ (1 − p ) k /4 ≤ exp(− k 19/20 /4); identical signatures are the same event. Both unions are o (1), so some choice of bits colours G with no monochromatic nonzero-step progression (Theorem 6.3). §7: base- q D digits with that cyclic colouring in every digit give W 2 m ( k ) &gt; q Dm — the lowest digit at which the step is nonzero is a cyclic progression with nonzero step — hence W r ( k ) &gt; q D ⌊log 2 r ⌋ ≥ k ck ⌊log 2 r ⌋ . The threshold on k is fixed in §2–§6 before r appears, which is the uniformity. The exponent 10 −5 is where the rich-case union is made to clear (the affine union clears for any fixed c ): 2 c must sit below γ/80 with γ = 1/100, and nothing in the paper claims it is close to the truth.

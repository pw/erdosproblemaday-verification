Let 𝑔⁡(𝑛)
 denote the number of groups of order 𝑛
. If 𝑛 ≤2𝑚
 then 𝑔⁡(𝑛) ≤𝑔⁡(2𝑚)
.
#1160: [Va99,5.71]
group theory
The open status of this problem reflects the current belief of the owner of this website. There may be literature on this problem that I am unaware of, which may partially or completely solve the stated problem. Please do your own literature search before expending significant effort on solving this problem. If you find any relevant literature not mentioned here, please add this in a comment.
This is listed as an open problem (Question 22.16) in [BNV07], which reports it as a 'quite natural conjecture, whose origin we have been unable to trace satisfactorily. We have heard it attributed at various times to various people, such as Paul Erdős and Graham Higman.'

Question 22.18 of [BNV07] suggests the even stronger conjecture
∑
𝑛<2𝑚𝑔⁡(𝑛)≤𝑔⁡(2𝑚)
for all sufficiently large 𝑚
 (perhaps even as soon as 𝑚 ≥7
).

Pantelidakis [Pa03] proved that the original conjecture is true if 𝑛
 is odd and 𝑚 ≥3619
.

Thanks. My numerology was in the direction of "Question 22.18 of [BNV07]", except I wanted it true for all 𝑚
. So one version might be that ∑2𝑚−1<𝑛<2𝑚𝑔⁡(𝑛) ≤𝑔⁡(2𝑚), which is true for small 𝑚 (and tight for 𝑚 =4 and 𝑚 =5). (This would imply the original Erdős conjecture above.)

The table you mention is easy to look at as a human. For a computer, the OEIS has a file for 𝑛 ≤2047 linked here. It's also available as FiniteGroupCount[n] in Mathematica.

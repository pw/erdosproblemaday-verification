WHERE THE PROOF GOES BEYOND THE STATE OF THE ART (test these hardest):
(i) The density stays p while the number of independent tests grows — the routing tree spends resolution without spending measure. Is Eρ(B) = p really exact and is B really a finite union of half-open cells per period (so measurable, periodic)?
(ii) Lemma 4.3's EXACT probability: are the (M−1)r selector addresses really distinct AND disjoint from every entry exposed in the atom ξ (ξ exposes one entry per selector table — the entry AT x, including tables off x's route)? Are the terminal addresses κ_{i,n} really pairwise distinct for every realization of the selectors (three cases in the proof)? Is the conditioning order (ξ, then the whole selector field A, then average) legitimate — is F_x ⊆ A, are terminal tables independent of A, does averaging (1−p)^{ΣA} over independent fair bits give (1−p/2)^{(M−1)r}? Does the argument silently need that no selector entry read by a translated point's route BELOW U is one exposed at x? (The route from U_i downward uses selectors in the child subtree at the translated point's keys — can any coincide with x's exposed entry in that same table? (iii) Lemma 4.2: the route of y_n(t) reaches U and rejects children 1..i−1 — this uses (3.3) for every edge preceding e_i in preorder. (iv) Lemma 5.1's scale count: Q_i(z) determined by J_{b*_{e_i}}(z) alone — does the test consult ANY table at resolution finer than b*_{e_i}? (v) The parameter order (5.4)–(5.5): M depends on p,q; d on M,p; K on M,d; g on K,c,p; r_* on M,q,p. Any hidden circularity (does g or r_0 feed back into K or M)?
